## Risk Management

### 1. Risk Management Worksheet
*Source: Module 6, Lesson 12 — Building Your Own Risk Rules*

A personal, written risk framework. The right values depend on your own
capital, strategy, experience, and risk tolerance — this course
deliberately doesn't fill them in for you.

```
Account: ___________________________
Maximum risk per trade: ____________
Maximum daily loss: ________________
Maximum weekly loss: _______________
Maximum simultaneous exposure: _____
Maximum consecutive losses before review: ____
Stop-loss rule: ____________________
Position-sizing rule: ______________
Conditions under which trading stops: ______
Review schedule: ___________________
```

*Reminder, from Module 6, Lesson 12: each field matters because it turns
an in-the-moment decision into one made calmly, in advance — maximum
risk per trade is your Lesson 2 percentage; maximum daily/weekly loss is
a circuit breaker beyond any single trade; maximum consecutive losses
before review is a specific pause point, not a permanent stop; and the
review schedule is how often you step back and evaluate results (Module
9), rather than only reacting trade by trade.*

### 2. Position-Sizing Worksheet **(reformatted)**
*Source: Module 6, Lesson 5 — Practical Position-Sizing Calculations*

```
Position Size = Amount Willing to Risk ÷ Risk Per Unit
```

Fill in your own numbers, then work the calculation step by step:

```
Account balance:              $____________
Risk percentage:              ____________ %
Risk amount (balance × risk %): $____________

Stop-loss distance (pips):    ____________
Pip value assumption:         $____________ per pip

Position size = Risk amount ÷ (Stop-loss distance × Pip value)
             = $__________ ÷ (__________ pips × $__________/pip)
             = ____________ (lots)
```

*Reminder, from Module 6, Lesson 5: account size, risk percentage,
stop-loss distance, and pip value are what determine position size —
confidence in the trade, or how much you want to make, are not inputs
to this formula. Recalculate for every trade; a stop-loss distance that
changes changes the position size too.*

### 6. Trading Schedule
*Source: Module 8, Lesson 10 — Trading Schedule and Routine*

**Before Session**

- [ ] Calendar check (Module 5, Lesson 9)
- [ ] Market review (current structure and context, per Lesson 4)
- [ ] Setup review (re-reading the written setup definitions from Lesson 5)
- [ ] Risk review (confirming the day's risk boundaries, per Lesson 8)

**During Session**

- [ ] Wait for a setup that meets the written criteria (Lesson 5)
- [ ] Validate entry against the written entry rules (Lesson 6)
- [ ] Execute according to the plan, with no in-the-moment exceptions
- [ ] Avoid impulsive changes to an open trade's stop or target (Lesson 7)

**After Session**

- [ ] Record the trade(s) taken, or the decision not to trade
- [ ] Review adherence — did today's decisions actually follow the written plan?
- [ ] Note any rule violations specifically (Module 7's Rule-Violation Log)
- [ ] Close the session deliberately

### 15. Bias/Error Checklist **(reformatted)**
*Source: Module 9, Lesson 10 — Avoiding Backtesting Biases and Errors
(the module's own Quick Reference names this "the nine items from
Lesson 10"; converted here to checkbox form using those same nine
items' exact labels).*

- [ ] Checked for **look-ahead bias** — did I avoid using information that wouldn't have been available at the time of the decision?
- [ ] Checked for **selection bias** — did I log every setup the rules produced, not just the clean-looking ones?
- [ ] Checked for **survivorship bias** — did I test the instruments my plan actually trades, across their full available history?
- [ ] Checked for **overfitting (curve-fitting)** — are my rules simple and reasoned, not tuned to maximize one backtest?
- [ ] Checked for **rule-changing mid-test** — did I finish testing one fixed version of the rules, with no changes partway through?
- [ ] Checked for **ignoring spread, commission, and slippage** — did I build a reasonable cost estimate into the results?
- [ ] Checked for **cherry-picking** — did I decide the rules and test period in advance, and record the honest result?
- [ ] Checked for **testing only in a favorable period** — did I include more than one type of market condition?
- [ ] Checked for **instrument-switching** — did I evaluate the instruments I originally chose, rather than searching for a better-looking pair?

*(Full what-it-is / how-it-happens / why-it-matters / countermeasure
explanation for each of the nine items is in Module 9, Lesson 10.)*

### 16. Testing Workflow
*Source: Module 9, Lesson 15 — Building Your Complete Testing &
Journaling System*

```
1. Confirm rules are specific and testable
2. Choose historical data (varied conditions)
3. Backtest manually, one candle at a time — no look-ahead
4. Log every trade with R-multiples
5. Calculate win rate, avg win, avg loss
6. Calculate expectancy and profit factor
7. Review equity curve for drawdown and streaks
8. Check against the Bias/Error Checklist
9. Forward test on demo, journal every trade
10. Review weekly and monthly; improve one change at a time
```

## Practical Development

### 17. Demo Trading Readiness Checklist
*Source: Module 10, Lesson 2 — Demo Trading and Practice*

- [ ] My trading plan (Module 8) is written and complete.
- [ ] I understand my own risk rules and can apply them without recalculating from scratch every time.
- [ ] I understand the order types (market, limit, stop) I intend to use.
- [ ] I understand exactly how I place a stop-loss and take-profit on my platform.
- [ ] I have a journal (Module 9) ready to record every demo trade.
- [ ] I understand my own entry-execution process, step by step.
- [ ] I check the economic calendar (Module 5) as part of my routine.
- [ ] I understand my plan's rules well enough to apply them without re-reading them mid-trade.
- [ ] I understand that this is a demo environment, and that live conditions can differ.

### 18. Broker Due-Diligence Checklist
*Source: Module 10, Lesson 5 — Broker and Platform Due Diligence*

- [ ] I have identified the exact legal entity name, not just the brand name.
- [ ] I have identified where that entity is legally registered.
- [ ] I have identified which regulator, if any, supervises that entity.
- [ ] I have independently verified the registration on the regulator's own official register.
- [ ] I understand the fees — spread, commission, and swap.
- [ ] I understand the withdrawal terms and any conditions attached.
- [ ] I understand the account restrictions that apply.
- [ ] I understand the dispute/complaint process.
- [ ] I have read (not skimmed) the terms and conditions.
- [ ] I understand what the published terms say about how client funds are held.

### 19. Scam Red-Flag Checklist
*Source: Module 10, Lesson 6 — Recognizing Forex Scams and Fraud*

- [ ] Guaranteed or "risk-free" returns are promised.
- [ ] Profit claims seem unrealistic relative to any transparent, verifiable evidence.
- [ ] There's pressure to deposit quickly, or an offer with an artificial deadline.
- [ ] Testimonials or account screenshots are the only evidence offered.
- [ ] The entity can't be verified on any regulator's official register.
- [ ] Account access or trading control is being requested directly.
- [ ] Recruiting others is part of how the offer pays out.
- [ ] Funds are requested to an individual's personal account, not a verified corporate channel.

*Treat any of these, especially more than one together, as a reason to
stop and verify independently before proceeding.*

### 20. Signal Provider Evaluation Checklist **(reformatted)**
*Source: Module 10, Lesson 7 — Signal Providers and Copy Trading
("How to evaluate a signal provider, if using one at all"; converted
here to checkbox form using those same criteria.)*

- [ ] **Transparency** — does the provider clearly explain their setup and reasoning, or only issue bare instructions?
- [ ] **Track record evidence** — is there a verifiable, complete record, ideally from an independently auditable source?
- [ ] **Risk disclosure** — does the provider disclose the actual risk involved, not just the potential reward?
- [ ] **Methodology** — is there an explainable, consistent approach, or does it change unpredictably?
- [ ] **Costs** — what does the service actually cost, and is that disclosed upfront?
- [ ] **Conflicts of interest** — does the provider profit from an affiliate relationship that could incentivize excessive signal frequency?
- [ ] **Reasoning explained** — does using this source build understanding, or only dependency?

### 24. 90-Day Development Roadmap
*Source: Module 10, Lesson 15 — Your Next 90 Days*

**Days 1–30 — Foundation, preparation, structured practice and documentation**

- [ ] Review this course's foundations for any area that still feels unclear.
- [ ] Finalize your Module 8 trading plan into a complete, written first draft.
- [ ] Practice chart reading and setup identification deliberately (Modules 2–4).
- [ ] Begin structured demo practice, using the Demo Trading Readiness Checklist first.
- [ ] Start your journal from the very first demo trade.

**Days 31–60 — Testing, review, refinement and execution development**

- [ ] Continue consistent demo or forward-testing practice.
- [ ] Review the journal weekly.
- [ ] Identify any recurring execution problems.
- [ ] Test one controlled improvement at a time, if the evidence supports it.

**Days 61–90 — Consistency, performance review and continued development**

- [ ] Review the full 90 days of accumulated evidence, not just the most recent results.
- [ ] Evaluate rule adherence honestly, using the Performance Review Template.
- [ ] Identify specific strengths and weaknesses in the process.
- [ ] Refine the plan based on that evidence.
- [ ] Decide on the appropriate next learning or practice step.

*This roadmap describes a development timeline, not a guaranteed
progression toward profitability or live-trading readiness — per Module
10, Lesson 15, whether to begin live trading after these 90 days depends
on the evidence gathered, financial circumstances, risk tolerance, and
personal judgment, not on a fixed calendar.*
