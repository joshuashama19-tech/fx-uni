# How to Use This Course

A practical guide to actually working through FX University — what to
do, in what order, and what each part of the course is for.

## The recommended process, module by module

1. **Study each lesson.** Read it once for the overall idea, then again
   for the specifics — the formulas, the worked example, the beginner
   mistake called out at the end.
2. **Understand the concept before moving forward.** If a lesson's idea
   isn't clear, re-read it or work through its example again before
   starting the next lesson — later lessons build on earlier ones.
3. **Complete the exercises.** Exercises apply what the lesson just
   covered, usually with fresh numbers or a new scenario, so you're
   practicing the skill, not just recalling the lesson.
4. **Take the knowledge checkpoint.** Answer every question in the
   module's quiz before looking at the answer key — checking as you go
   defeats the purpose of a checkpoint.
5. **Review incorrect answers.** For anything you missed, go back to the
   specific lesson the answer key points you to, rather than just
   noting the correct answer and moving on.
6. **Complete applicable worksheets and checklists.** Starting in Module
   6, most modules include a fillable worksheet, checklist, or template
   — use it for real, with your own numbers and your own plan, not as a
   reading exercise.
7. **Practice concepts in a controlled environment.** Where a module
   calls for it (backtesting in Module 9, demo trading in Module 10),
   actually do it before moving on, rather than treating the description
   as a substitute for the practice itself.
8. **Keep records of your development.** Your own trading plan, journal,
   and review notes (Modules 8–10) are meant to accumulate across the
   whole course, not be thrown away module to module.
9. **Review previous material when necessary.** If a later module
   references an earlier one and the reference isn't clear, go back and
   re-read it — the course is built to be cross-referenced this way on
   purpose.
10. **Progress through the modules sequentially.** Modules 1 → 10, in
    order. Later modules are written assuming the earlier ones are
    already done.

## What each part of the course is for

- **Lessons** teach one concept at a time, in plain language first, then
  with its formal terminology — the core teaching content of every
  module.
- **Examples** show a lesson's concept applied to a specific, worked,
  realistic case, so the idea isn't left abstract.
- **Exercises** give you practice applying a module's concepts yourself,
  usually with new numbers or a new scenario — most have no single
  "correct" answer key, since the point is the process, not a lookup.
- **Quizzes** are a short knowledge checkpoint at the end of each module
  — a signal for what to review, not a pass/fail test.
- **Answer keys** are kept in a separate file from each quiz on purpose,
  so you can answer honestly before checking, with a brief explanation
  for each answer.
- **Checklists** are an honest, concrete "before you move on" check —
  check a box only if you could actually do it right now, not just
  recall reading about it.
- **Worksheets and templates** (starting in Module 6) are the fillable
  tools — position sizing, your trading plan, your backtesting log, your
  journal — that turn what you've learned into your own working
  documents. The full set is compiled together in the Practical Resource
  Library near the end of the course.
- **The development roadmap** (Module 10) is the practical plan for what
  comes after you finish the course — not a countdown to any particular
  outcome, but a structure for continuing to practice, test, and review.

## A note on pace

This course doesn't set a fixed timeline. Some students move through a
module in a single sitting; others take a module a week, particularly
once exercises, backtesting, and demo practice are involved starting in
Module 9. Either is fine — the sequence matters more than the speed.
