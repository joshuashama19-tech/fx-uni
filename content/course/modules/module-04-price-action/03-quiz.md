# Module 4 — Knowledge Checkpoint

15 questions covering everything in Module 4. Mixed format: multiple
choice, true/false, short answer, and interpretation scenarios. Write
down your answers before checking `04-answer-key.md` — checking as you go
defeats the point of a checkpoint.

**1. Multiple choice.** Compared to indicator-based analysis, price
action is best described as:

A) A more certain, more reliable method that replaces the need for
   indicators
B) A different lens that reads raw price movement directly, carrying the
   same underlying uncertainty as indicator-based tools
C) A shortcut that removes the need to think about risk
D) A method that only works on higher timeframes

**2. Short answer.** A trader says: "The candles got smaller and the
upper wicks got longer, so buyers are running out of strength." Which
part of this sentence is an observation, and which part is an
interpretation?

**3. Short answer.** Define a swing high and a swing low, in your own
words.

**4. Calculation.** AUD/USD prints the following swing points, in order:
a swing low at **0.65200**, a swing high at **0.65450**, a swing low at
**0.65350**, and a swing high at **0.65600**.

Is the second swing low higher or lower than the first? Is the second
swing high higher or lower than the first? What kind of structure does
this describe?

**5. Interpretation scenario.** A trader marks a demand zone after price
consolidated in a tight range and then rallied away sharply. Price later
returns to that same zone and falls straight through it without any
reaction. Does this mean the trader identified the zone incorrectly?
Explain using Lesson 5.

**6. True or False.** Every time price briefly spikes past a recent swing
high or low before reversing, it's proof that a specific participant
deliberately targeted the resting orders at that level.

**7. Calculation.** USD/JPY has been forming an uptrend. The most recent
swing high sits at **149.800**. Price then closes at **149.950**.
Calculate the break size in pips, and state whether this is a bullish or
bearish Break of Structure.

**8. Interpretation scenario.** EUR/USD has established bullish
structure: a swing low at **1.31000**, a swing high at **1.31300**, a
higher low at **1.31150**, and a higher high at **1.31500**. Price then
prints a lower high at **1.31400**, and shortly after, closes at
**1.31100** — below the prior higher low of 1.31150.

Does this close confirm a Change of Character? By how many pips does it
break the prior swing low? Does this confirm the market has now reversed
into a downtrend? Explain.

**9. Short answer.** What is the difference between a confirmation entry
and an anticipation entry? Is either one universally "correct"?

**10. Calculation.** A trader enters short on GBP/USD at **1.27300**,
places a stop loss at **1.27450**, and sets a target of **1.27000**.
Calculate the risk in pips, the reward in pips, and the resulting
risk-to-reward ratio.

**11. True or False.** A trade plan with a 1:3 risk-to-reward ratio is
guaranteed to be profitable over time, as long as the trader is patient.

**12. Interpretation scenario.** A trader's 4-hour chart shows clear
bullish structure. Their 1-hour chart just printed a bearish CHOCH
against that context. What is a reasonable way to handle this
disagreement, per Lesson 11? What would *not* be a reasonable way to
handle it?

**13. Short answer.** List at least four of the ten steps in the
repeatable price-action framework from Lesson 12, in order.

**14. True or False.** The first Change of Character in a trend is proof
that the trend has fully reversed.

**15. Interpretation scenario.** Using the framework from Lesson 12,
briefly describe how you would approach a chart where the daily timeframe
shows bearish structure, price on the 4-hour chart rallies into a
previously marked supply zone, and then prints a bearish Break of
Structure at that zone. What would make you treat this as a
higher-confidence idea, and what would it still not guarantee?
