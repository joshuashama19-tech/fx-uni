# Module 4 — Exercises

These exercises apply what you covered in `01-content.md`. They use new
scenarios and numbers, not the exact examples from the lessons, so you're
applying the method, not recalling a result. Work through them in order.
Answers aren't provided here on purpose (as with Modules 1–3): this is
practice, not another quiz.

None of these exercises ask you to predict what price will do next — price
action, as taught in this module, is a way of reading and planning, not a
forecasting tool. Every exercise asks you to interpret, classify, or plan
from information that's already given.

## Exercise 1 — Observation versus interpretation

A trader describes a chart: "Price printed three large bullish candles in
a row with almost no upper wicks. Then the next two candles were smaller,
with longer upper wicks, even though each one still closed slightly
higher than the last. Buyers are clearly running out of energy and a
reversal is coming."

1. Which part of this description is **observation** (something you could
   point to directly on the chart)?
2. Which part is **interpretation**?
3. Is the interpretation definitely correct just because the observation
   is accurate? Explain, using Lesson 2.

## Exercise 2 — Classifying structure

USD/CAD prints the following swing points, in order: a swing high at
**1.35800**, a swing low at **1.35500**, a swing high at **1.35650**, and
a swing low at **1.35300**.

1. Is the second swing high higher or lower than the first? Is the second
   swing low higher or lower than the first?
2. Based on Lesson 4, is this bullish structure, bearish structure, or
   neither? Explain using the HH/HL or LH/LL pattern.

## Exercise 3 — Identifying a supply or demand zone

AUD/JPY trades in a tight range between **89.500** and **89.550** for
several sessions, then falls sharply, losing more than 150 pips over the
next few days with very little pause.

1. Is the **89.500–89.550** area a supply zone or a demand zone? Explain
   using Lesson 5's definitions.
2. Per Lesson 5, if price later returns to this zone, does that guarantee
   a reaction there? Explain why or why not.
3. What specific claim about this zone would Lesson 5 tell you *not* to
   make?

## Exercise 4 — Equal lows and a liquidity sweep

GBP/USD prints a swing low at **1.24300**, then a second swing low at
**1.24290** a few sessions later.

1. Are these close enough to be read as equal lows, per Lesson 6?
2. Price then drops to **1.24250** — briefly trading below both swing
   lows — before reversing and closing back up at **1.24380**, above
   both original lows. Describe this full sequence as an **observation**
   only, without yet explaining why it might have happened.
3. Now give one **interpretation** of that same sequence, and explain, in
   one sentence, why the interpretation isn't something you can verify
   just by looking at the chart.

## Exercise 5 — Identifying a Break of Structure

NZD/USD has been forming bullish structure: a swing low at **1.17200**, a
swing high at **1.17450**, and a pullback swing low at **1.17320** (a
higher low). Price then rallies and closes at **1.17500**.

1. Does this close confirm a Break of Structure? If so, in which
   direction, and by how many pips does it break the prior swing high?
2. Per Lesson 7, would a single wick reaching 1.17500 without a close
   there count the same way? Explain.

## Exercise 6 — Identifying a Change of Character

EUR/USD has been in established bearish structure: a swing high at
**1.09800**, a swing low at **1.09500**, a lower high at **1.09650**, and
a lower low at **1.09300**. Price then attempts a new low but only
reaches **1.09350** before rallying and closing at **1.09700**.

1. Is **1.09350** higher or lower than the prior swing low of 1.09300?
   What does Lesson 8 call this kind of warning sign?
2. Does the close at 1.09700 confirm a Change of Character? Explain which
   prior swing point it breaks, and by how many pips.
3. Does this CHOCH prove the market has now reversed into an uptrend?
   Explain using Lesson 8.

## Exercise 7 — Confirmation vs. anticipation

Two traders watch the same setup develop. Trader A enters as soon as
price starts approaching a swing low, expecting a CHOCH that hasn't
happened yet. Trader B waits until price actually closes below that swing
low before entering.

1. Which trader is using a confirmation entry, and which is using an
   anticipation entry?
2. Per Lesson 9, is one of these approaches simply "correct" and the
   other "wrong"? Explain the trade-off each one accepts.

## Exercise 8 — Placing a logical stop loss

A trader plans a short entry based on a bearish CHOCH. The CHOCH was
confirmed by a close below a prior swing low, and the most recent swing
high (the one that would need to hold for the bearish idea to stay valid)
sits at **1.18400**.

1. Per Lesson 10, should the stop loss be placed at a fixed distance the
   trader always uses, or referenced to a specific price level? Which
   level, and why?
2. Explain, in your own words, why an arbitrarily-placed stop (for
   example, "always 50 pips") can be dangerous even if it happens to be
   profitable on some trades.

## Exercise 9 — Calculating risk-to-reward

A trader enters long on EUR/USD at **1.10200** after a confirmed bullish
CHOCH. They place a stop loss at **1.10050** (below the invalidation
point) and a target at **1.10500** (the prior significant swing high).

1. Calculate the risk in pips.
2. Calculate the reward in pips.
3. Calculate the risk-to-reward ratio.
4. Per Lesson 10, does this ratio, by itself, tell you whether the trade
   is a good idea? Explain.

## Exercise 10 — Handling conflicting timeframes

A trader's 4-hour chart shows clear bullish structure. Their 1-hour chart
just printed a bearish CHOCH against that context.

1. Per Lesson 11, should the trader treat the 1-hour CHOCH as an
   automatic signal to go short, since it's the most recent signal?
   Explain.
2. Describe two reasonable ways a price-action trader might respond to
   this specific conflict, per Lesson 11.

## Exercise 11 — Spot the mistake

Each short scenario below contains one of the beginner mistakes covered
in this module. Identify the mistake and briefly explain what the trader
should have done differently.

1. "I marked a zone around every small pause on the chart, so now I have
   about fifteen overlapping supply and demand zones."
2. "Price spiked two pips above yesterday's high and immediately reversed
   — that's definitely smart money hunting stops."
3. "The market made a slightly higher high, so I'm calling this a
   confirmed uptrend now."
4. "I saw the setup and got in immediately — it just looked right."
5. "My higher timeframe shows a downtrend, but I only mentioned that in
   my notes when it happened to agree with the short trade I wanted to
   take anyway."

## Exercise 12 — Applying the full framework

Using the 10-step framework from Lesson 12, describe (in your own words,
5–8 sentences) how you would approach the following scenario: the daily
chart shows bearish structure. On the 4-hour chart, price rallies into a
previously marked supply zone that also sits near two equal highs from
several weeks earlier. At that zone, price prints a lower high and then
closes below the prior swing low.

Walk through what you'd check at each relevant step, what your entry,
invalidation, and target might be based on, and why following this
process doesn't guarantee the trade works out.
