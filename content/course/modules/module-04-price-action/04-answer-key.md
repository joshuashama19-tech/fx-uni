# Module 4 — Answer Key

Kept in a separate file from `03-quiz.md` on purpose, so the quiz can be
worked through honestly before checking. Question numbers match exactly.

**1. Answer: B) A different lens that reads raw price movement directly,
carrying the same underlying uncertainty as indicator-based tools.**
Price action doesn't replace the need to think about risk (ruling out C)
and isn't limited to higher timeframes (ruling out D) — and it is
explicitly *not* more certain than indicator-based analysis (ruling out
A) (Lesson 1).

**2. Answer guide.** "The candles got smaller and the upper wicks got
longer" is the **observation** — something directly visible on the
chart. "Buyers are running out of strength" is the **interpretation** — a
reasonable explanation for that observation, but not something you can
verify just by looking (Lesson 2).

**3. Answer guide.** A **swing high** is a point where price reaches a
local peak — higher than the price immediately before and after it —
before turning back down. A **swing low** is the mirror: a local trough,
lower than the price immediately before and after it, before price turns
back up (Lesson 3).

**4. Answer: the second swing low (0.65350) is higher than the first
(0.65200); the second swing high (0.65600) is higher than the first
(0.65450). This is bullish structure (higher highs and higher lows).**
Both conditions — higher highs *and* higher lows — are met, which is
required to call structure bullish (Lesson 4).

**5. Answer guide.** No. Supply and demand zones are areas of interest
based on past observed behavior, not guaranteed reversal points — Lesson
5 is explicit that plenty of zones get revisited and pass straight
through without any reaction at all. A zone passing through cleanly
doesn't mean it was identified incorrectly; it means this particular
instance simply didn't produce a reaction, which is a normal and expected
outcome, not an error (Lesson 5).

**6. Answer: False.** Not every wick beyond a level is a deliberate
liquidity hunt, and "smart money" is not a group whose intentions can be
read from a chart with certainty. Some moves beyond a level are a
sweep-and-reverse; others are normal volatility, a genuine breakout that
continues, or news-driven movement unrelated to resting orders at all
(Lesson 6).

**7. Answer: 15 pips. This is a bullish Break of Structure.** 149.950 −
149.800 = 0.150, and for a JPY pair one pip is the second decimal place
(0.01), so 0.150 ÷ 0.01 = 15 pips. Because the market was already forming
an uptrend and price closed beyond the most recent swing high in that
same direction, this confirms continuation — a bullish BOS (Lesson 7).

**8. Answer: Yes, this confirms a Change of Character, by 5 pips.**
1.31150 − 1.31100 = 0.00050 = 5 pips. **This does not confirm the market
has reversed into a downtrend.** A CHOCH is the first break against
prevailing structure — a signal worth close attention — but Lesson 8 is
explicit that it is not proof of a full reversal. Structure could shift
into a range instead, or the original uptrend could resume; some traders
wait for further confirmation, such as a subsequent bearish BOS, before
treating the shift as established (Lesson 8).

**9. Answer guide.** A **confirmation entry** waits until the trigger
condition has actually occurred (for example, a close beyond a level)
before entering. An **anticipation entry** acts before that condition
happens, based on an expectation that it will. Neither is universally
correct: anticipation can capture a better price if the idea plays out,
at the cost of being wrong more often when it doesn't; confirmation gives
up some of that price in exchange for more evidence the idea already
happened (Lesson 9).

**10. Answer: risk = 15 pips, reward = 30 pips, risk-to-reward = 1:2.**
Risk: 1.27450 − 1.27300 = 0.00150 = 15 pips. Reward: 1.27300 − 1.27000 =
0.00300 = 30 pips. 30 ÷ 15 = 2, written as a 1:2 ratio (Lesson 10).

**11. Answer: False.** A risk-to-reward ratio only describes the plan
*if* the target is reached — it says nothing about how often a trade idea
like this actually reaches its target versus its stop. A trader can plan
a high ratio on every trade and still lose money overall if the stop is
hit meaningfully more often than the target (Lesson 10).

**12. Answer guide.** A reasonable response treats the lower-timeframe
CHOCH as **lower-confidence** given it conflicts with the higher-timeframe
context — for example, waiting to see if the higher timeframe eventually
shows its own CHOCH, or treating the 1-hour signal only as a reason for
caution on an existing position rather than a fresh countertrend entry.
It would **not** be reasonable to simply ignore the higher-timeframe
context and treat the 1-hour CHOCH as an automatic green light to trade
against the bigger picture — that's the "timeframe shopping" mistake
Lesson 11 warns against (Lesson 11).

**13. Answer guide.** Any four of, in order: (1) identify market context,
(2) identify current structure, (3) mark relevant areas, (4) identify
liquidity/important price areas, (5) wait for a defined structural
condition, (6) define entry, (7) define invalidation/stop loss, (8)
define target, (9) record the trade idea, (10) review the result (Lesson
12).

**14. Answer: False.** A CHOCH is the first break against prevailing
structure and a signal worth watching closely — it is explicitly not
proof that a trend has fully reversed. Structure can shift into a range,
or the original trend can resume after a single CHOCH (Lesson 8).

**15. Answer guide.** A reasonable approach: identify context (bearish,
daily), identify structure and mark the supply zone as a relevant area,
then treat the confirmed bearish BOS at that zone as the structural
condition to act on — defining an entry trigger, an invalidation point
above the zone/recent swing high, and a target at the next significant
structure level. This would be treated as a higher-confidence idea
because multiple independent things line up (higher-timeframe bearish
context, a marked supply zone, and a confirmed BOS all agreeing) — but
per Lesson 12, following the framework carefully and having more evidence
line up still doesn't guarantee the trade works out; it only makes the
*process* consistent (Lesson 12).
