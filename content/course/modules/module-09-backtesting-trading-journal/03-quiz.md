# Module 9 — Knowledge Checkpoint

18 questions covering everything in Module 9. Mixed format: multiple
choice, true/false, short answer, calculations, and scenario-based
questions. A calculator is genuinely useful for several of these. Write
down your answers before checking `04-answer-key.md` — checking as you go
defeats the point of a checkpoint.

**1. Multiple choice.** Backtesting is best described as:

A) A guarantee that a strategy will be profitable in the future
B) Applying a specific, written set of trading rules to historical price
   data to see what results those rules would have produced
C) A way to eliminate all uncertainty about a trading strategy
D) A substitute for ever trading on a demo account

**2. True or False.** A positive backtest proves a strategy will be
profitable going forward.

**3. Short answer.** Explain what look-ahead bias is, in your own words.

**4. Scenario.** While manually backtesting, a trader scrolls the chart
forward, notices price rallied sharply a few candles later, and then goes
back to mark a "valid setup" at the earlier candle because they already
know it worked out. What specific error is this, and why does it make the
backtest's results meaningless as evidence?

**5. Calculation.** A trader's planned risk on a trade is $40. The trade
closes at a result of −1.5R. What is the dollar result?

**6. Calculation.** A backtest produces 24 total trades, 11 of them
winners. Calculate the win rate (to one decimal place).

**7. Calculation.** A strategy's results show a win rate of 50%, an
average win of $30, and an average loss of $18. Calculate the expectancy
per trade.

**8. Calculation.** A backtest produces $450 in gross profit and $300 in
gross loss. Calculate the profit factor.

**9. Multiple choice.** A backtest's largest drawdown is found by:

A) Subtracting the final result from the starting balance
B) Finding the largest decline from a peak in the running equity curve
   down to the lowest point reached before a new peak is set
C) Averaging every losing trade's result
D) Counting the total number of losing trades

**10. Short answer.** A backtest produces this sequence of results:
win, loss, loss, loss, win, loss, loss, win, loss, loss, loss, loss, win.
What is the longest losing streak in this sequence?

**11. True or False.** This course states a specific number of trades
(such as 30, or 100) that makes any backtest statistically proven.

**12. Short answer.** Name any three of the nine biases or errors covered
in Lesson 10.

**13. Scenario.** A trader tests the same set of rules on six different
currency pairs, one after another, and keeps only the pair that produced
the best-looking result, presenting that one as "the backtest." Which
bias or error does this describe?

**14. Multiple choice.** Per Lesson 11's Trading Journal Template, the
"during the trade" section is mainly used to record:

A) The final dollar result of the trade
B) Whether the plan was followed exactly as written, and any adjustments
   made along with their stated reason
C) The instrument's typical spread
D) The trader's account balance at the start of the month

**15. Short answer.** Explain, in one or two sentences, why filling in a
trading journal without ever reviewing it weekly or monthly provides very
little of its actual value.

**16. True or False.** Per Lesson 13, this course recommends changing
several rules of a strategy at the same time when responding to
disappointing results, so the whole process moves faster.

**17. Scenario.** A trader has backtested a strategy across a reasonably
large, varied sample of historical data, with a positive expectancy and a
manageable drawdown. They have never traded the strategy in real time.
Per Lesson 14, what should this trader do next, and why?

**18. True or False.** Completing this module's backtest and journal
setup means a trading strategy is proven and ready for live trading with
real money.
