# Module 5 — Knowledge Checkpoint

15 questions covering everything in Module 5. Mixed format: multiple
choice, true/false, short answer, and economic-data scenarios. Write down
your answers before checking `04-answer-key.md` — checking as you go
defeats the point of a checkpoint.

**1. Multiple choice.** Fundamental analysis is best described as:

A) A method that replaces the need for technical analysis or price action
B) The study of the economic and political conditions that influence a
   currency's value, used alongside technical analysis and price action
C) A way of predicting the exact price of a currency in advance
D) Only useful for very long-term investors, never for short-term traders

**2. Short answer.** In an economic report, what do "previous," "forecast,"
and "actual" each mean?

**3. Calculation.** A CPI report shows: Previous 2.4%. Forecast 2.5%.
Actual 2.9%. Calculate the surprise relative to forecast, and the change
relative to the previous reading.

**4. True or False.** An economic report that comes in exactly as
forecast typically produces a larger market reaction than one that
significantly surprises the forecast.

**5. Short answer.** Why is "higher interest rates always strengthen a
currency" an oversimplification? Name at least one reason from Lesson 3.

**6. Multiple choice.** Core CPI differs from headline CPI because it:

A) Only measures housing costs
B) Excludes food and energy prices, aiming to show the more persistent
   underlying trend
C) Is calculated using a completely different currency
D) Is always higher than headline CPI

**7. Calculation.** An unemployment rate report shows: Previous 4.2%.
Forecast 4.2%. Actual 4.0%. Calculate the surprise relative to forecast,
and state whether this is generally read as a stronger or weaker
labor-market signal.

**8. Calculation.** An NFP report shows: Previous 190,000. Forecast
200,000. Actual 160,000. Calculate the difference between actual and
forecast, and the difference between actual and the previous reading.

**9. True or False.** A strong NFP report that beats its forecast
guarantees that the related currency will strengthen.

**10. Calculation.** A GDP report shows: Previous quarter (annualized)
2.0%. Forecast 2.2%. Actual 1.7%. Calculate the surprise relative to
forecast.

**11. Short answer.** Name at least one limitation of GDP as an economic
indicator, per Lesson 7.

**12. Short answer.** Besides the rate decision itself, name two other
things a central bank communicates that traders watch closely, per Lesson
8.

**13. Interpretation scenario.** Using this sample calendar row — USD,
CPI y/y, Previous 2.8%, Forecast 2.9%, Actual 2.6% — is this an upside or
downside surprise relative to forecast? Is inflation accelerating or
decelerating compared to the previous reading?

**14. True or False.** A specific type of geopolitical event (for
example, a trade dispute) always produces the exact same currency
reaction whenever it happens.

**15. Interpretation scenario.** A currency's recent inflation and
employment data have both surprised to the upside, and its central bank's
guidance has turned more cautious about inflation than expected.
Separately, that currency's chart shows bearish market structure with no
recent bullish Break of Structure. Per Lesson 12, do the fundamental and
technical pictures agree here? What should a trader following this
module's framework do with that situation?
