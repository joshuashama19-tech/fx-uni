# Module 5 — Completion Checklist

## Before You Move On

Go through this honestly — check each box only if you could actually do
it right now, without flipping back to the lesson. Module 6 builds on
this module's mindset around uncertainty, but doesn't re-teach the
economic concepts here.

### Core understanding

- [ ] I can explain what fundamental analysis is, and how it works
      alongside technical analysis and price action rather than replacing
      either.
- [ ] I can explain what "previous," "forecast," and "actual" each mean,
      and I understand that the surprise relative to forecast usually
      matters more than the raw number.

### Rates, inflation, and employment

- [ ] I can explain what an interest rate is and why central banks set
      them, and I can explain why "higher rates always strengthen a
      currency" is an oversimplification.
- [ ] I can explain inflation and CPI in plain language, and I understand
      the difference between headline and core readings.
- [ ] I can explain the main employment indicators (unemployment rate,
      payrolls, wage growth) and why they matter to an economy.
- [ ] I can explain what NFP is, why it tends to create volatility, and
      why a strong or weak NFP report doesn't guarantee a specific
      currency reaction.

### GDP, central banks, and the calendar

- [ ] I can explain what GDP measures and at least one of its
      limitations as a standalone indicator.
- [ ] I can explain the general role a central bank plays — policy
      decisions, statements, press conferences, and forward guidance —
      without relying on any specific current rate or officeholder.
- [ ] I can read a sample economic calendar and explain what each column
      (date/time, currency, event, previous/forecast/actual, impact)
      means.

### News, geopolitics, and synthesis

- [ ] I understand how major economic news can create volatility, wider
      spreads, and execution risk, without treating every release as
      identical.
- [ ] I can name at least two channels through which a geopolitical
      event can affect a currency, and I understand why the reaction
      isn't consistent or predictable from one event to the next.
- [ ] I can describe the framework for combining fundamental context with
      technical analysis and price action, and I know what to do when the
      two disagree.

### Practice completed

- [ ] I completed all 12 exercises in `02-exercises.md`.
- [ ] I completed the 15-question checkpoint in `03-quiz.md` before
      looking at the answers.
- [ ] I checked my answers against `04-answer-key.md` and re-read any
      lesson connected to a question I missed.

### Mindset check

- [ ] I understand that no economic report, central-bank decision, or
      geopolitical event in this module guarantees a specific currency
      outcome.
- [ ] I understand that markets price in expectations, that data gets
      revised, and that correlations between fundamentals and price can
      change over time.
- [ ] I understand that being right about the economy does not
      automatically mean being right about a trade, and I have not used
      this module's material to justify skipping the risk management
      Module 6 covers.

---

If every box above is checked honestly, you're ready for **Module 6 —
Risk Management**. If anything is still unclear, that's a normal part of
learning this material — go back to the relevant lesson in
`01-content.md` before moving forward. There's no benefit to continuing
on a shaky foundation.
