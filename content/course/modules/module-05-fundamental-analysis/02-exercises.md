# Module 5 — Exercises

These exercises apply what you covered in `01-content.md`. They use new
scenarios and numbers, not the exact examples from the lessons, so you're
applying the method, not recalling a result. Work through them in order.
Answers aren't provided here on purpose (as with Modules 1–4): this is
practice, not another quiz.

None of these exercises ask you to predict a future price. Fundamental
analysis, as taught in this module, is a way of reading and interpreting
information that's already published — not a forecasting tool for price
itself.

## Exercise 1 — Reading previous, forecast, and actual

A country's monthly retail sales report is due. Previous: +0.4%.
Forecast: +0.3%. The actual figure comes in at +0.3%, exactly matching
the forecast.

1. Is this a surprise relative to what the market expected?
2. Per Lesson 2, would you expect this release to produce a large or a
   small immediate reaction? Explain why.
3. Does the fact that it matched the forecast tell you anything about
   whether retail sales are accelerating or decelerating compared to last
   month? Explain, using both comparisons from Lesson 2.

## Exercise 2 — CPI surprise and trend

A CPI report shows: Previous 2.8%. Forecast 2.9%. Actual 2.6%.

1. Calculate the surprise relative to forecast (actual − forecast).
2. Calculate the change relative to the previous reading (actual −
   previous).
3. Is this a hotter or cooler inflation surprise than expected? Is
   inflation accelerating or decelerating compared to last month?
4. Per Lesson 4, does this reading, by itself, guarantee that the
   currency will move in a specific direction? Explain.

## Exercise 3 — Headline vs. core inflation

The same report in Exercise 2 also shows core CPI (ex food and energy) at
2.9%, against the headline reading of 2.6%.

1. Calculate the gap between headline and core (headline − core).
2. Per Lesson 4, what might it suggest if headline inflation is running
   *below* core inflation — for example, that food or energy prices fell
   even while underlying inflation stayed firmer?
3. Why might a central bank pay closer attention to the core figure than
   the headline figure in a case like this?

## Exercise 4 — Interpreting an NFP miss

An NFP report shows: Previous 210,000. Forecast 195,000. Actual 150,000.

1. Calculate the difference between actual and forecast.
2. Calculate the difference between actual and the previous reading.
3. Is this a stronger or weaker labor-market report than expected?
4. Per Lesson 6, does a weak NFP report like this guarantee the currency
   weakens? What other information (mentioned in Lesson 6) would you want
   to check before drawing a conclusion?

## Exercise 5 — Unemployment rate surprise

An unemployment rate report shows: Previous 3.8%. Forecast 3.8%. Actual
4.1%.

1. Calculate the surprise relative to forecast.
2. Is a higher-than-forecast unemployment rate generally read as a
   stronger or weaker labor-market signal?
3. Per Lesson 5, why does comparing the actual value to the forecast
   matter more than just noting the rate went up?

## Exercise 6 — Rate expectations vs. the decision itself

A central bank is widely expected to raise its policy rate by 0.25
percentage points at its next meeting. At the meeting, it raises the rate
by exactly that amount.

1. Per Lessons 2 and 3, would you expect this decision, on its own, to
   produce a large market reaction? Explain why.
2. Now suppose the accompanying statement signals the bank is unlikely to
   raise rates further for the rest of the year, more dovish than the
   market expected. Per Lesson 8, could this move the currency even
   though the rate decision itself matched expectations? Explain.

## Exercise 7 — Interpreting a GDP report

A GDP report shows: Previous quarter (annualized) 1.5%. Forecast 1.6%.
Actual 1.2%.

1. Calculate the surprise relative to forecast.
2. Calculate the change relative to the previous quarter.
3. Per Lesson 7, name one limitation of GDP data that means this single
   report shouldn't be read as the complete picture of the economy's
   health.

## Exercise 8 — Reading a sample economic calendar

| Date | Time (UTC) | Currency | Event | Impact | Previous | Forecast | Actual |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 09:00 | EUR | Retail Sales m/m | Medium | +0.2% | +0.1% | +0.1% |
| Tue | 12:30 | USD | CPI y/y | High | 2.8% | 2.9% | 2.6% |
| Wed | 11:45 | EUR | Interest Rate Decision | High | 3.75% | Hold | Hold |
| Thu | 12:30 | USD | GDP q/q (annualized) | Medium | 1.5% | 1.6% | 1.2% |

1. Which currency has two high or medium-impact releases in this
   calendar window?
2. Which single release in this table would you flag as the biggest
   surprise relative to its forecast, and why?
3. Per Lesson 9, name one reason a trader might check this calendar
   before opening a new position on Tuesday morning.

## Exercise 9 — Geopolitical scenario analysis

A country unexpectedly announces new sanctions against a major trading
partner. In the hours that follow, that country's currency weakens
against a currency commonly perceived as a safer holding during uncertain
periods.

1. Per Lesson 11, name at least two of the general channels through which
   a geopolitical event like this could affect a currency.
2. Does this specific example prove that sanctions announcements always
   weaken the announcing country's currency? Explain, using Lesson 11's
   caution about causality.

## Exercise 10 — Combining fundamentals and technicals

A currency's fundamental backdrop looks supportive: recent inflation data
has surprised to the upside, and the central bank's latest guidance
leaned more cautious about inflation than expected. Separately, that
currency's chart shows bearish market structure (Module 4) with no recent
bullish Break of Structure.

1. Per Lesson 12, does the fundamental backdrop and the technical picture
   agree here?
2. What would Lesson 12's framework suggest a trader do with this kind of
   disagreement, rather than picking whichever picture supports a trade
   they already want to take?

## Exercise 11 — Spot the mistake

Each short scenario below contains one of the beginner mistakes covered
in this module. Identify the mistake and briefly explain what the trader
should have done differently.

1. "CPI came in at 3.2%, that's high, so I'm selling the currency right
   now."
2. "The central bank held rates exactly as expected, so nothing about
   this meeting matters."
3. "NFP beat forecast, so the currency has to go up."
4. "I don't check the calendar — I just trade whatever the chart shows,
   whenever I want."
5. "I was right that inflation would come in hot, so the trade was bound
   to work."

## Exercise 12 — Applying the practical workflow

Using the seven-step workflow from Lesson 14, describe (in your own
words, 5–8 sentences) how you would approach the following situation: you
check the calendar and see an interest-rate decision is scheduled
tomorrow for a currency you follow, with the market widely expecting a
hold. The most recent CPI report for that currency beat its forecast, and
the most recent employment report also beat its forecast. The daily chart
shows bullish structure.

Walk through what you'd check at each relevant step, and explain why
completing this workflow still wouldn't guarantee a specific outcome.
