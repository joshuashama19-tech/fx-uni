# Module 2 — Exercises

These exercises apply what you covered in `01-content.md`. They're built
to test interpretation and judgment, not just definitions — several ask
you to explain your reasoning, not just state an answer. Work through
them in order. Answers aren't provided here on purpose (as with Module
1): this is practice, not another quiz.

## Exercise 1 — Reading full candles

For each candle below, state whether it's bullish or bearish, and
calculate its body size, upper wick, lower wick, and range (in pips).

1. EUR/USD, 1-hour: Open 1.09200, High 1.09260, Low 1.09120, Close
   1.09150.
2. EUR/USD, 1-hour: Open 1.09150, High 1.09300, Low 1.09140, Close
   1.09280.
3. GBP/USD, 4-hour: Open 1.26400, High 1.26430, Low 1.26370, Close
   1.26410.

## Exercise 2 — What the shape is telling you

Using candle 1 from Exercise 1 (the bearish one) and candle 3 (the small
bullish one): in 2–3 sentences, explain what the *shape* of each candle
(not just its color) suggests happened during that period — specifically,
what the wick sizes tell you about the contest between buyers and
sellers.

## Exercise 3 — A JPY candle, and why it matters

A 4-hour USD/JPY candle shows: Open 150.200, High 150.550, Low 150.150,
Close 150.480. Calculate its body, upper wick, lower wick, and range in
pips — being careful to use the correct pip convention for this pair.
Then explain, in one sentence, what would have gone wrong if you'd used
the same decimal-place rule you'd use for EUR/USD.

## Exercise 4 — Reading a sequence, not just candles

You're shown four consecutive 1-hour GBP/USD candles, described in
order: (1) a large bearish candle with almost no wicks, (2) a small
candle with a tiny body sitting near the bottom of candle 1's range, (3)
another small, similar candle just above candle 2, (4) a large bullish
candle that closes back above where candle 1 started. In 2–4 sentences,
describe the story this sequence tells — not just "bearish, small,
small, bullish," but what it suggests about the shift between sellers
and buyers.

## Exercise 5 — Timeframe reasoning

1. A trader looks only at a 1-minute EUR/USD chart and sees what looks
   like a sharp, dramatic move. Explain what they should check on a
   higher timeframe before assuming that move is significant, and why.
2. In your own words, explain what happens to the same hour of price
   action when you view it as a single 1-hour candle versus twelve
   separate 5-minute candles. What information is gained, and what is
   lost, in each view?

## Exercise 6 — Trend or range?

For each set of swing highs and lows (in chronological order), state
whether the market is in an uptrend, a downtrend, or a range, and briefly
justify your answer using the HH/HL or LH/LL pattern.

1. Highs: 1.31200 → 1.31450 → 1.31700 → 1.31900. Lows: 1.30800 →
   1.31050 → 1.31300 → 1.31550.
2. Highs: 0.65800 → 0.65600 → 0.65350 → 0.65100. Lows: 0.65400 →
   0.65200 → 0.64950 → 0.64700.
3. Highs: 1.08900 → 1.08920 → 1.08880 → 1.08910. Lows: 1.08600 →
   1.08580 → 1.08610 → 1.08590.

## Exercise 7 — Identifying a level

A trader notices that over three separate weeks, USD/CAD has approached
1.37500 on four different occasions, and each time, price turned back
down within a few pips without closing meaningfully above it.

1. What would you call this level, and why does it matter?
2. Is it reasonable to assume price will turn down at 1.37500 a fifth
   time? Explain your answer.

## Exercise 8 — Breakout or false breakout?

For each scenario, state whether it looks like a genuine breakout, a
false breakout, or whether there isn't enough information to tell yet —
and explain your reasoning.

1. Price wicks briefly above a well-established resistance level
   intra-candle, then the candle closes back below the level.
2. Price closes three consecutive candles above a well-established
   resistance level, and continues climbing over the following day.
3. Price closes a single candle just barely above a resistance level,
   with no further candles yet to confirm what happens next.

## Exercise 9 — Multi-timeframe context

The daily EUR/USD chart shows a clear uptrend (a sustained sequence of
higher highs and higher lows over several weeks). On the 15-minute
chart, the most recent few hours show a short sequence of lower highs
and lower lows.

1. Read in isolation, what would the 15-minute chart alone suggest is
   happening?
2. Read together with the daily chart, what's a more complete
   interpretation — and why is this a good example of why multi-timeframe
   analysis matters?

## Exercise 10 — Describing market structure

In 3–5 sentences, describe the overall "structure" of a market that: has
been making higher highs and higher lows for two weeks, has a resistance
level at a specific price it has failed to close above three times, and
has just printed its first lower high after those two weeks of climbing.
What would you want to watch for next, and why can't you be certain what
happens from here?

## Exercise 11 — Spot the mistake

Each short scenario below contains one of the beginner mistakes covered
in this module. Identify the mistake and briefly explain what the trader
should have done differently.

1. "I saw two bullish candles in a row and called it an uptrend."
2. "Price broke above the resistance level I'd been watching, so I
   assumed it was a genuine breakout and didn't consider it could
   reverse."
3. "I drew my support level at exactly 1.09000 and got confused when
   price reacted at 1.08970 instead."
4. "I only checked the 5-minute chart before deciding the market was
   trending down."
