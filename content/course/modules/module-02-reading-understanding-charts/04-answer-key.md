# Module 2 — Answer Key

Kept in a separate file from `03-quiz.md` on purpose, so the quiz can be
worked through honestly before checking. Question numbers match exactly.

**1. Answer: B) A summary of the open, high, low, and close for one
fixed period of time.** A candlestick isn't a prediction (A), an
average (C), or a single trade (D) — it's a compressed summary of
everything that happened during one period (Lesson 2).

**2. Answer guide.** Open = the price when the period began. High = the
highest price reached during the period. Low = the lowest price reached
during the period. Close = the price when the period ended (Lesson 3). A
correct answer just needs all four defined accurately, in any wording.

**3. Answer: Bearish. Body = 7 pips.** Close (1.09330) is below open
(1.09400), so the candle is bearish. Body = |1.09330 − 1.09400| =
0.00070 = 7 pips (Lesson 4, Lesson 5).

**4. Answer: Upper wick = 6 pips. Lower wick = 3 pips. Range = 16
pips.** Upper wick = High − the higher of open/close = 1.09460 −
1.09400 = 0.00060 = 6 pips. Lower wick = the lower of open/close − Low =
1.09330 − 1.09300 = 0.00030 = 3 pips. Range = High − Low = 1.09460 −
1.09300 = 0.00160 = 16 pips (Lesson 5).

**5. Answer: False.** A bullish candle only tells you price closed
higher than it opened during that specific period — it's not a promise
about what happens next (Lesson 4).

**6. Answer guide.** This sequence suggests a strong initial bullish move
that has lost momentum and is pausing (consolidating) near its highs,
rather than continuing to climb or immediately reversing. The next
candle matters because it can resolve the pause in either direction — a
strong bullish candle breaking to new highs would suggest continuation,
while a strong bearish candle giving back the gains would suggest
sellers have taken control. Neither outcome is guaranteed by the pause
itself (Lesson 6).

**7. Answer guide.** Switching to a daily timeframe shows more history
and a smoother, more compressed picture — good for seeing the bigger
trend — but it hides the hour-by-hour detail of exactly how price moved
within each day (spikes, reversals, the order events happened in). The
1-hour chart shows that detail but less overall history at a glance
(Lesson 7).

**8. Answer: C) An uptrend.** The highs are strictly increasing
(1.31200 → 1.31450 → 1.31700) and the lows are strictly increasing
(1.30800 → 1.31050 → 1.31300) — a clear higher-highs-and-higher-lows
sequence (Lesson 8).

**9. Answer: False.** A range is defined by the *absence* of a clear
directional sequence — swing highs and lows that cluster and bounce
between a rough ceiling and floor, rather than climbing or falling in
sequence (Lesson 9).

**10. Answer guide.** Support is a price level where a market has
repeatedly stopped falling and turned back up. It tends to form because
enough traders have made decisions around that level before — buying
near a price that held previously — and that collective pattern tends to
produce similar reactions there again (Lesson 10).

**11. Answer: C) Price moves beyond a level, appears to break it, then
reverses back to the other side.** That's the defining feature of a
false breakout — it looks like a genuine break until it reverses
(Lesson 11).

**12. Answer: False.** No characteristic of a breakout — including a
closing candle beyond the level — guarantees the move continues. Genuine
and false breakouts can look identical while they're happening (Lesson
11).

**13. Answer guide.** Market structure is the overall shape formed by a
market's swing highs and lows over time — essentially trend direction,
the key support/resistance levels in play, and whether those levels have
recently held or broken, read together as one picture rather than
separate topics (Lesson 12).

**14. Answer guide.** The most reasonable interpretation is that the
15-minute pullback (lower highs and lower lows) is likely a short-term
move within the larger daily uptrend, not necessarily a reversal of it —
though this is a read of the current picture, not a certainty. Looking
only at the 15-minute chart in isolation could make it look like a new
downtrend has started, missing the broader context the daily chart
provides. This is exactly why multi-timeframe analysis matters (Lesson
13).

**15. Answer: C) Assuming any move beyond a level is automatically a
valid breakout.** This is the beginner mistake called out directly in
Lesson 11. The other options (A, B, D) all describe good practices
taught in this module, not mistakes.
