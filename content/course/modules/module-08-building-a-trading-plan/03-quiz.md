# Module 8 — Knowledge Checkpoint

17 questions covering everything in Module 8. Mixed format: multiple
choice, true/false, short answer, and scenario-based questions. These
test whether you understand how to build and apply a written trading
plan, not just recall a definition. Write down your answers before
checking `04-answer-key.md` — checking as you go defeats the point of a
checkpoint.

**1. Multiple choice.** A trading plan is best described as:

A) A single trading idea about one specific trade
B) A written, specific set of rules covering every major trading
   decision, from what to trade through how to review performance
C) A guarantee that a trader will be profitable if followed
D) The same thing as a trading strategy

**2. Short answer.** Explain the difference between a strategy and a
complete trading plan, per Lesson 1.

**3. Short answer.** Explain the difference between a trading plan and a
single trading idea, per Lesson 1.

**4. Multiple choice.** Compared to day trading, swing trading typically
involves:

A) Shorter holding periods and more setups per day
B) Longer holding periods, higher timeframes, and less frequent
   monitoring
C) The exact same timeframes and holding period, just a different name
D) No use of higher-timeframe context at all

**5. True or False.** This course teaches that one trading style
(scalping, day trading, swing trading, or position trading) is superior
to the others.

**6. Short answer.** Name at least two reasons a beginner might benefit
from limiting their watchlist to a small number of instruments, per
Lesson 3.

**7. Scenario.** A trader wants to build a Context → Setup → Entry
timeframe framework for swing trading. Suggest one reasonable timeframe
for each of the three roles, and explain in one sentence what each
timeframe is used to determine.

**8. True or False.** Switching between timeframes mid-analysis to find
one that supports a trade the trader already wants to take is a
legitimate application of top-down analysis.

**9. Short answer.** Explain the difference between a required condition
and an optional condition in a setup definition, per Lesson 5.

**10. Scenario.** A trader's written entry rule says: "enter when the
setup looks promising." Is this rule objective and checkable, per Lesson
13's test? Rewrite it as a more observable condition.

**11. Multiple choice.** Per Lesson 6's Entry Checklist, which of the
following should be confirmed before executing a trade?

A) Only that the entry trigger has occurred
B) Market context, setup conditions, entry trigger, news/calendar, risk,
   and stop-loss location all together
C) Only that the trader feels confident about the trade
D) Only the position size, independent of everything else

**12. Short answer.** Per Lesson 7, explain why moving a stop loss or
take profit because of how a trade currently feels undermines a written
plan.

**13. True or False.** This course prescribes a single universal risk
percentage that every trading plan must use.

**14. Scenario.** A trader's setup meets every entry criterion, but a
high-impact news release for the traded currency is scheduled in fifteen
minutes, and the trader's plan states no new entries within 30 minutes of
such a release. What should the trader do, and why?

**15. Scenario.** A trader identifies what otherwise looks like a
promising setup, but cannot find a clear, logical point to place an
invalidation-based stop loss. Per Lesson 11, is this a valid reason to
pass on the trade? Explain.

**16. Short answer.** List at least four of the fourteen components in
Lesson 12's Complete Trading Plan Template.

**17. True or False.** Once a trading plan has passed Lesson 14's Final
Trading Plan Audit, it can be considered a finished, proven system that
no longer needs testing.
