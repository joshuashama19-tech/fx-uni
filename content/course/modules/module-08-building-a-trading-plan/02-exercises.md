# Module 8 — Exercises

These exercises are the actual workshop this module promised — most of
them ask you to write your own rules, not just answer a question about
someone else's. Work through them in order; by the end, you'll have
draft material for most of your own first trading plan. Answers aren't
provided here on purpose (as with Modules 1–7): there's no single correct
answer to most of these, only whether your own rules are specific and
checkable.

## Exercise 1 — Choosing your trading style

Using Lesson 2's comparison table, choose a trading style (or a blend of
two adjacent ones) that fits your own available time, schedule, and
experience level. Write two or three sentences explaining your choice,
specifically referencing your own time constraints — not which style
sounds the most appealing in the abstract.

## Exercise 2 — Market selection worksheet

Complete Lesson 3's Market Selection Worksheet for at least two
instruments you'd consider trading. Fill in every column (Instrument, Why
Trade It, Session, Main Timeframe, Typical Conditions, Notes) with
specific answers, not placeholders.

## Exercise 3 — Timeframe selection

Using Lesson 4's Context → Setup → Entry framework, write out the
specific three timeframes you'd use, matched to the trading style you
chose in Exercise 1. Explain, in one sentence per timeframe, what
question that timeframe answers in your process.

## Exercise 4 — Defining a setup

Complete Lesson 5's Setup Definition Worksheet for one specific setup,
drawing on concepts from Modules 2–5. Be specific about which conditions
are required and which are optional confluence — a setup with everything
marked "required" is a warning sign worth reconsidering.

## Exercise 5 — Writing objective entry rules

Write a complete entry rule for the setup you defined in Exercise 4,
covering: the exact entry trigger, whether it's a confirmation or
anticipation entry (Module 4, Lesson 9), and whether it uses a market or
pending order. Then apply Lesson 13's test: could someone else, reading
only this rule, apply it the same way you would?

## Exercise 6 — Writing exit rules

Write a complete stop-loss and take-profit rule for the same setup.
State specifically how the stop-loss location is determined (not a fixed
pip distance untethered to structure) and which take-profit method
(Lesson 7) your plan uses.

## Exercise 7 — Creating risk rules

Write the risk section of your plan: your maximum risk per trade, your
maximum simultaneous exposure, and your rule for whether the position-
sizing calculation (Module 6, Lesson 5) gets run before every single
entry. Do not perform the calculation itself here — state the rule that
ensures it happens.

## Exercise 8 — Creating news rules

Choose one of Lesson 9's four approaches to news (avoiding certain
windows, waiting for volatility to settle, including news in the setup,
or treating it as context only), or a variation of your own, and write it
as a specific, written rule — precise enough that you could check,
afterward, whether you followed it on a specific day.

## Exercise 9 — Creating a trading schedule

Using Lesson 10's Before / During / After structure, write your own
schedule, with at least three specific actions under each phase (not
generic statements like "stay focused").

## Exercise 10 — Creating no-trade rules

Using Lesson 11's No-Trade Checklist as a starting point, write at least
five specific no-trade conditions for your own plan — conditions specific
enough that you could check, in the moment, whether one currently
applies.

## Exercise 11 — Identifying vague rules

Each rule below is taken from a hypothetical trader's written plan.
Identify which ones are genuinely objective and checkable, and which ones
are vague, per Lesson 13's test.

1. "Enter when the setup looks clean."
2. "Enter only after price closes above the prior swing high on the
   1-hour chart."
3. "Exit if I feel like the trade isn't working."
4. "Exit at a 1:2 risk-to-reward ratio from entry."
5. "Avoid trading during high-impact news for the traded currency."
6. "Skip the trade if it doesn't feel right."

## Exercise 12 — Converting vague rules into observable conditions

Rewrite each vague rule you identified in Exercise 11 into a specific,
observable condition. There's no single correct rewrite — the goal is
that your version could be applied identically by two different readers.

## Exercise 13 — Completing the trading-plan template

Using Lesson 12's Complete Trading Plan Template and your answers from
Exercises 1–10, assemble a full first draft of your own trading plan.
Fill in all fourteen sections — leaving a section blank is a signal to
revisit the relevant lesson before moving on.

## Exercise 14 — Performing a final plan audit

Apply Lesson 14's Final Trading Plan Audit to the plan you assembled in
Exercise 13. For any item you can't honestly check off, write one
sentence identifying specifically what's missing or unclear, and which
lesson to revisit to fix it.
