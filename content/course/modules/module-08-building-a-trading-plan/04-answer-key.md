# Module 8 — Answer Key

Kept in a separate file from `03-quiz.md` on purpose, so the quiz can be
worked through honestly before checking. Question numbers match exactly.

**1. Answer: B) A written, specific set of rules covering every major
trading decision, from what to trade through how to review performance.**
A trading plan is broader than a single idea (ruling out A); it doesn't
guarantee profitability (ruling out C); and it's broader than a strategy
alone, which is only the market-reading logic (ruling out D) (Lesson 1).

**2. Answer guide.** A strategy is the market-reading logic — the
specific technical, price-action, or fundamental conditions that define a
setup. A complete trading plan includes the strategy, but also the risk
rules, behavioral rules, schedule, news rules, and no-trade conditions
built around it (Lesson 1).

**3. Answer guide.** A trading idea is a single, one-off view about one
specific trade. A trading plan is the repeatable process that produces
and evaluates many trading ideas over time, using the same criteria
consistently (Lesson 1).

**4. Answer: B) Longer holding periods, higher timeframes, and less
frequent monitoring.** Per Lesson 2's comparison table, swing trading
typically holds positions for days to weeks on 4-hour to daily
timeframes, with moderate rather than constant monitoring (Lesson 2).

**5. Answer: False.** Lesson 2 is explicit that this course does not
claim any one trading style is superior — the right choice depends on a
trader's available time, schedule, personality, and experience (Lesson
2).

**6. Answer guide.** Any two of: it lets a trader actually get to know a
pair's typical behavior, session patterns, and typical spread rather than
dividing attention across many; it avoids diluting the careful analysis
Modules 2–4 teach across too many charts at once; and it reduces the
number of news calendars and correlated positions a trader has to track
at the same time (Lesson 3).

**7. Answer guide.** Any reasonable combination consistent with Lesson
4's framework — for example: Context = daily chart (determines overall
trend/structure direction); Setup = 4-hour chart (determines whether a
specific setup, like a pullback to a marked zone, is present); Entry =
1-hour chart (determines whether a specific entry trigger has actually
formed) (Lesson 4).

**8. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 4, this is exactly the "timeframe
shopping" mistake — switching timeframes specifically to find one that
supports a decision already made undermines the entire point of a fixed,
consistent Context → Setup → Entry framework (Lesson 4).

**9. Answer guide.** A required condition must be present for the setup
to be valid at all — its absence rules the trade out automatically. An
optional condition (supporting confluence) strengthens a setup when
present but isn't independently necessary for the setup to be considered
valid (Lesson 5).

**10. Answer: No, this rule is not objective or checkable** — "looks
promising" depends on the reader's own judgment and could be applied
differently by two different people, or by the same trader on two
different days. **A more observable rewrite** (one reasonable example):
"enter when price closes beyond a marked structural level in the
direction of higher-timeframe context, with the required confluence
conditions from my setup definition present" (Lesson 6, applying Lesson
13's test).

**11. Answer: B) Market context, setup conditions, entry trigger,
news/calendar, risk, and stop-loss location all together.** Lesson 6's
Entry Checklist is explicit that all of these should be confirmed before
executing, not just the entry trigger alone (A), a feeling of confidence
(C), or position size in isolation (D) (Lesson 6).

**12. Answer guide.** A written plan exists specifically to move
decisions out of the emotionally-charged moment of an open trade and into
a calmer moment decided in advance (per Module 7). Moving a stop or
target because of how the trade currently feels reintroduces exactly the
in-the-moment, emotionally-driven decision-making the plan was built to
prevent, and changes the trade's actual risk or reward without a fresh,
deliberate decision to accept that change (Lesson 7, building on Module
6, Lesson 3).

**13. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 8 (and Module 6, Lesson 2), this course
treats the specific risk percentage as a decision each trader makes for
their own plan — commonly 0.5%, 1%, or 2% — not a single mandatory
universal number (Lesson 8).

**14. Answer: The trader should pass on this specific entry.** Per Lesson
9, the written news rule was decided calmly, in advance, specifically for
moments like this one — applying it here is the rule working as intended,
not a missed opportunity. A different, equally legitimate plan could have
a different written news rule, but this trader's own stated rule applies
directly to this situation (Lesson 9).

**15. Answer: Yes, this is a valid reason to pass.** Per Lesson 11, "stop-
loss location is unclear" is explicitly listed as a no-trade condition —
if a logical invalidation point can't be identified, the trade's actual
risk can't be properly defined either (Module 6), regardless of how
promising the setup otherwise looks (Lesson 11).

**16. Answer guide.** Any four of: trading objective, trading style,
markets/pairs, timeframes, setup, entry rules, stop-loss rules,
take-profit/exit rules, risk rules, news rules, trading schedule,
no-trade rules, psychology/behavior rules, review process (Lesson 12).

**17. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 14, a plan that passes the audit is a
proper, specific first draft — not a validated, proven system. It still
needs to be tested and refined using Module 9's backtesting and
journaling process before its actual results are known (Lesson 14).
