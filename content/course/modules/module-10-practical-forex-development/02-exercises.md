# Module 10 — Exercises

These exercises apply what you covered in `01-content.md`. Several of
them use hypothetical situations rather than a single correct number —
this module is about judgment and process, not just calculation.
Answers aren't provided here on purpose (as with prior modules): most of
these have no single correct answer, only whether your reasoning is
specific and grounded in what this module actually taught.

## Exercise 1 — Your demo-trading readiness checklist

Complete Lesson 2's Demo Trading Readiness Checklist for yourself,
honestly. For any box you can't check yet, write one specific, concrete
action that would let you check it.

## Exercise 2 — Applying your trading plan

Using Lesson 3's before/during/after structure, write out exactly what
you would do in each of the three stages for your own next planned
trading session — not in general terms, but the specific steps you would
actually take.

## Exercise 3 — Evaluating a hypothetical broker

A broker's website advertises "regulated, tight spreads, and fast
withdrawals," with a regulator's logo displayed in the footer.

1. Using Lesson 5's ten questions, list what you would need to check
   before opening an account with this broker.
2. Specifically, how would you verify the regulatory claim, rather than
   trusting the logo?

## Exercise 4 — Identifying scam red flags

For each hypothetical offer below, name at least one specific warning
sign from Lesson 6, and explain what it suggests:

1. "Join our program: guaranteed 8% return every month, zero risk."
2. A trading "mentor" reaches out first on social media, offering to
   place trades directly on your account if you share your login.
3. An investment group's only evidence of results is a folder of
   screenshots showing large winning trades.

## Exercise 5 — Evaluating a hypothetical signal provider

A signal provider charges a monthly fee and sends entries by message,
with no explanation of the reasoning behind each one, and a results page
showing only winning trades.

1. Using Lesson 7's evaluation criteria, list what's missing or
   concerning about this provider.
2. What would you want to see instead, before considering using a
   signal source like this at all?

## Exercise 6 — Evaluating a hypothetical prop-firm offer

An evaluation program advertises a profit target and a maximum drawdown
limit, but its rules on holding trades over a weekend and around news
releases aren't mentioned on the sign-up page.

1. Per Lesson 8, why does this matter before paying for the evaluation?
2. What would you do before signing up?

## Exercise 7 — Assessing operational execution risks

Describe a specific scenario (real or realistic) in which each of the
following could occur, and explain how Lesson 10's Execution Checklist
would help catch it before the order is submitted:

1. Entering the wrong position size.
2. Entering the wrong direction.
3. Placing a stop-loss at the wrong price.

## Exercise 8 — Assessing financial readiness (hypothetical trader)

A hypothetical trader has six months of demo practice, a written and
backtested trading plan, and consistent rule adherence in their journal.
They are considering funding a live account with money they've set aside
specifically for a car repair they expect to need within the next two
months.

1. Per Lesson 9's three kinds of readiness, which one(s) does this
   trader appear to have?
2. Which one is clearly a problem here, and why?

(This exercise is about applying the course's own readiness framework to
a hypothetical case — it is not financial advice, and no single
"correct" dollar amount or timeline is expected.)

## Exercise 9 — Reviewing a hypothetical performance report

A trader's one-month performance report shows: 22 trades taken, a
positive net result, but only 14 of those 22 trades actually met every
required condition in their written setup — the other 8 were taken
because "the market felt like it was moving."

1. Using Lesson 11's review categories, what would you flag in this
   report beyond the fact that the month was profitable?
2. Why does a profitable month not automatically mean the process was
   sound?

## Exercise 10 — Identifying strategy-hopping behavior

A trader backtested a strategy across 90 trades with a positive
expectancy (Module 9). After 4 live losing trades in a row, they abandon
it and switch to a different setup they saw discussed online.

1. Is this strategy-hopping, per Lesson 12? Explain using the specific
   numbers in this scenario.
2. What should this trader have done instead?

## Exercise 11 — Building your continuous-improvement cycle

Using Lesson 12's review cycle (Collect → Measure → Review → Identify →
Change → Retest), describe how you would apply each step to your own
trading process over your next full month of practice.

## Exercise 12 — Locating yourself on the roadmap

Using Lesson 13's ten-stage roadmap, honestly identify which stage your
own progress is currently at, and name one specific action that would
move you toward the next stage.

## Exercise 13 — Your personal 90-day development roadmap

Using Lesson 15's structure (Days 1–30, 31–60, 61–90), write your own
90-day plan, naming specific actions for each phase rather than copying
the module's example list unchanged.

## Exercise 14 — Final course integration exercise

In a few paragraphs, connect at least four different modules from this
course (for example, Module 6's risk rules, Module 7's psychology
frameworks, Module 8's trading plan, and Module 9's journal) into a
single description of how you personally intend to apply this course
going forward. Be specific about your own plan, your own risk rules, and
your own review process — this is the closest thing this course has to a
final exam, and there's no single correct answer.
