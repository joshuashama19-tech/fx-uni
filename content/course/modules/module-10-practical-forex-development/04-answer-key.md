# Module 10 — Answer Key

Kept in a separate file from `03-quiz.md` on purpose, so the quiz can be
worked through honestly before checking. Question numbers match exactly.

**1. Answer: B) Because executing a plan consistently, under real
conditions, is a separate skill from understanding it.** Knowledge
doesn't become outdated on this timescale (ruling out A), demo trading
isn't a broker requirement (ruling out D), and this course's content
being complete is exactly why practice, not more content, is the missing
piece (ruling out C) (Lesson 1).

**2. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 2, demo trading is genuinely useful
practice, but execution conditions and emotional pressure can both differ
from live trading — it is evidence, not a guarantee (Lesson 2).

**3. Answer guide.** Any three of: trading plan completed, risk rules
understood, order types understood, stop-loss/take-profit process
understood, journal ready, entry-execution process understood, economic
calendar checked as routine, plan rules understood well enough to apply
without re-reading, understanding that live conditions can differ from
demo (Lesson 2).

**4. Answer guide.** Any two of: review the relevant sections of the
trading plan, check the economic calendar, identify current market
conditions on the context timeframe, prepare the watchlist (Lesson 3).

**5. Answer: C) A meaningful factor, but not a complete guarantee on its
own.** Per Lesson 4, regulation matters but doesn't eliminate every
possible problem, and it matters for accounts of every size, not only
large ones (ruling out A, B, and D) (Lesson 4).

**6. Answer guide.** A badge or claim on a broker's own website is not
independent verification — the broker controls that website and has an
incentive to display it regardless of accuracy. The claimed legal entity
name should instead be checked directly against the relevant regulator's
own official public register (Lesson 5).

**7. Answer guide.** Any two of: guaranteed returns, "risk-free"
framing, and (implicitly) an unrealistic return given no legitimate
trading approach can promise either guaranteed profit or zero risk
(Lesson 6).

**8. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 6, a professional website and active
social media presence are inexpensive to produce and available to
fraudulent operations just as easily as legitimate ones — they establish
nothing on their own (Lesson 6).

**9. Answer guide.** Learning from a provider's reasoning means
understanding the setup and logic behind a trade well enough to evaluate
or apply it independently. Copying entries blindly means acting on the
instruction alone, with no understanding of why it was given, no ability
to judge whether the same reasoning still applies to the next signal, and
no independent skill if the source stops or changes (Lesson 7).

**10. Answer: C) Any signal source can be evaluated using specific
criteria, and should never replace independent understanding.** This
course does not ban signals outright (ruling out A), does not recommend
blind copying including position size (ruling out B), and signal
provision isn't restricted to licensed brokers in this course's framing
(ruling out D) (Lesson 7).

**11. Answer guide.** Any three of: rules, drawdown limits, daily loss
limits, profit targets, time restrictions, consistency rules, payout
conditions, fees, account restrictions (Lesson 8).

**12. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 8, an evaluation tests performance
against one specific rule set over a comparatively short period — this
course explicitly does not treat passing one as proof of long-term
trading ability (Lesson 8).

**13. Answer: Financial readiness.** Per Lesson 9, this trader appears to
have knowledge readiness and process readiness, but risking money needed
for a near-term necessary expense (rent) means financial readiness is
missing, regardless of skill level (Lesson 9).

**14. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 9, this course deliberately does not
give a universal number of demo days or trades that guarantees
readiness — individual circumstances, strategies, and available time vary
too much for a single number to be honest (Lesson 9).

**15. Answer guide.** Any four of: correct instrument, correct direction,
correct position size, correct stop-loss level, correct take-profit/exit
level, spread acceptable, news conditions checked, correct order type,
trade matches the written plan (Lesson 10).

**16. Answer: No, a profitable result alone is not enough to call it a
well-executed month.** Per Lesson 11, a full review covers rule
adherence and setup quality alongside the raw result — a third of trades
not meeting the written setup's conditions is a specific, meaningful
process problem, even though this particular month happened to be
profitable overall (Lesson 11).

**17. Answer guide.** Evidence-based improvement responds to a specific
pattern found across a meaningful sample of results, reviewed
deliberately (Lesson 11's review cycle). Abandoning a strategy after one
losing trade is a reaction to a single outcome that a strategy's own
prior testing may already show is normal variation — it isn't based on
any actual pattern in the evidence (Lesson 12).

**18. Answer: False.** Per Lesson 15, whether to begin live trading after
90 days depends on the evidence gathered, financial circumstances, risk
tolerance, and personal judgment — this course does not recommend it
automatically for every student (Lesson 15).
