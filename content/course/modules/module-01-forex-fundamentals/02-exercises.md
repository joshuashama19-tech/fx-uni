# Module 1 — Exercises

These exercises apply what you just covered in `01-content.md`. Work
through them in order — several build on the answer from the one before.
Answers aren't provided here on purpose: this is practice, not another
quiz. If you're unsure about one, re-read the related lesson before
guessing.

## Exercise 1 — Base and quote currency

For each pair below, write down the base currency and the quote
currency.

1. USD/CAD
2. GBP/JPY
3. AUD/USD
4. EUR/GBP
5. USD/CHF

## Exercise 2 — Classify the pair

Classify each pair as **major**, **minor**, or **exotic**.

1. EUR/USD
2. EUR/GBP
3. USD/TRY
4. USD/JPY
5. AUD/NZD
6. USD/ZAR

## Exercise 3 — Reading bid/ask and calculating spread

For each quote below, identify the bid, the ask, and calculate the
spread in pips.

1. EUR/USD: 1.09210 / 1.09224
2. GBP/USD: 1.27185 / 1.27204
3. USD/JPY: 150.115 / 150.148

## Exercise 4 — Pip calculations

Calculate how many pips the price moved in each case. Watch for the JPY
pair — it doesn't use the same decimal place as the others.

1. EUR/USD moves from 1.09210 to 1.09280.
2. GBP/USD moves from 1.27300 to 1.27150.
3. USD/JPY moves from 150.100 to 150.430.
4. AUD/USD moves from 0.65500 to 0.65620.

## Exercise 5 — Lot size and pip value

Using the approximate pip values from Lesson 7 (standard lot ≈ $10/pip,
mini lot ≈ $1/pip, micro lot ≈ $0.10/pip, for a USD-quoted pair):

1. A trader opens 2 standard lots of EUR/USD. Price moves 15 pips in
   their favor. Approximately how much did they gain?
2. A trader opens 0.5 mini lots (5,000 units) of GBP/USD. Price moves 8
   pips against them. Approximately how much did they lose?
3. A trader has a $500 account and opens 1 standard lot of EUR/USD. A
   routine 25-pip adverse move happens. What percentage of the account
   does that represent? What does that tell you about the position size
   relative to the account?

## Exercise 6 — Simple position value

Calculate the full notional (total) value of each position, using the
formula: **units × price = position value**.

1. 1 standard lot (100,000 units) of EUR/USD at 1.08500. (Give your
   answer in the quote currency, USD.)
2. 1 mini lot (10,000 units) of USD/JPY at 149.500. (Give your answer in
   the quote currency, JPY.)
3. 3 micro lots (3,000 units) of GBP/USD at 1.26800. (Give your answer in
   the quote currency, USD.)

## Exercise 7 — Identify the order type

For each scenario, state whether the trader should use a market order, a
buy limit, a sell limit, a buy stop, or a sell stop.

1. A trader wants to enter immediately, at whatever price is currently
   available.
2. EUR/USD is at 1.08500. A trader wants to buy, but only if price first
   drops to 1.08300.
3. USD/JPY is at 149.500. A trader wants to sell if price breaks
   *downward* through 149.200, confirming a move lower.
4. GBP/USD is at 1.26800. A trader wants to sell, but only if price first
   rises to 1.27000.
5. A trader already has an open buy position and wants to automatically
   close it if price falls to a specific level below their entry.

## Exercise 8 — Leverage and margin

1. A broker offers 50:1 leverage. A trader wants to open 1 standard lot
   of EUR/USD at a price of 1.09000. What is the full notional value of
   the position, and approximately how much margin is required?
2. Using the same numbers as above, explain in your own words (2–3
   sentences) why the margin required is different from the amount of
   money the trader could actually lose on the trade.

## Exercise 9 — Trading sessions

1. Name the four major Forex trading sessions in the order they open.
2. Which overlap is generally considered the most active for major
   pairs, and why?
3. What is one practical reason a trader might avoid opening a new trade
   during a very low-liquidity period, like right around a session close
   or a major holiday?

## Exercise 10 — Spot the mistake

Each short scenario below contains one of the beginner mistakes covered
in this module. Identify the mistake and briefly explain what the trader
should have done differently.

1. "I chose 2 standard lots because it felt like a normal number to
   trade, without checking what that meant in dollars against my account
   size."
2. "I bought USD/JPY and figured out my stop loss distance the same way
   I always do on EUR/USD — just counting the same number of decimal
   places."
3. "I placed the trade and figured I'd decide where to get out once I
   saw how it was moving."
4. "I picked my broker because their signup bonus was the biggest one I
   found, and didn't look into anything else about them."

## Exercise 11 — Market participants

1. Name at least three types of participants in the Forex market, in
   roughly descending order of their typical share of trading volume.
2. In 1–2 sentences, explain why retail sentiment (forums, social media,
   influencers) is a weak basis for a trading decision on a major pair
   like EUR/USD.
