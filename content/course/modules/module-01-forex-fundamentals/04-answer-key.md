# Module 1 — Answer Key

Kept in a separate file from `03-quiz.md` on purpose, so the quiz can be
worked through honestly before checking. Question numbers match exactly.

**1. Answer: A) US Dollar.** In any pair, the base currency is always the
one listed first. USD/CHF: base is USD, quote is CHF.

**2. Answer: False.** Forex is decentralized — there is no single
exchange. It operates through a global network of banks, brokers, and
financial institutions (Lesson 2).

**3. Answer: C) EUR/GBP.** A minor pair (cross) pairs two major
currencies without the US Dollar. EUR/USD and USD/JPY are majors (they
include USD); USD/TRY is an exotic (it pairs USD with a smaller/emerging
economy's currency).

**4. Answer guide.** The bid is the price at which you can sell the base
currency right now; the ask is the price at which you can buy it right
now. The ask is always slightly higher than the bid, and the difference
between them is the spread (Lesson 5). A correct answer just needs to
capture "bid = sell price, ask = buy price, ask is higher."

**5. Answer: 1.4 pips.** 1.09354 − 1.09340 = 0.00014 = 1.4 pips.

**6. Answer: 39 pips.** For JPY pairs, a pip is the second decimal place.
148.610 − 148.220 = 0.390 = 39 pips.

**7. Answer: C) $10.00.** A standard lot (100,000 units) is worth
approximately $10 per pip on a USD-quoted pair (Lesson 7).

**8. Answer: False.** A buy limit is placed *below* the current price —
you're waiting to buy at a more favorable (lower) price. A **buy stop**
is placed above the current price (Lesson 8).

**9. Answer: A market order.** A market order executes immediately at
the current available price, which is exactly what this scenario calls
for (Lesson 8).

**10. Answer: B) Control a larger position size than their deposited
capital alone would allow.** Leverage does not eliminate risk (A), does
not affect the spread (C), and a broker (or equivalent regulated access
point) is still required (D) (Lesson 9).

**11. Answer: True.** Leverage magnifies gains and losses equally — it
changes how much a given price move affects the account, in both
directions (Lesson 9, Risk note).

**12. Answer guide.** Sydney, Tokyo (Asian), London (European), and New
York (US), in that order. The **London/New York overlap** is generally
considered the most active window for major pairs, because participants
from both high-volume regions are trading simultaneously (Lesson 10).

**13. Answer: B) Commercial and investment banks.** They're the largest
participants by volume, trading for clients and for themselves. Retail
traders (A, C) are a real but relatively small share of total volume,
and corporations paying overseas suppliers (D) trade for commercial
reasons but don't approach bank-level volume (Lesson 11).

**14. Answer: approximately $200, or 20% of the account.** 20 pips ×
$10/pip (1 standard lot) = $200. $200 ÷ $1,000 account = 20%. (This is
also a useful preview of why Module 6 exists — that's a large percentage
of an account to risk on one routine price move.)

**15. Answer guide.** Any concrete, practical reason is correct — for
example: a regulated broker is required to keep client funds in
segregated accounts, separate from the company's own operating funds;
regulation typically requires minimum capital and reporting standards;
and a regulated broker is accountable to a real financial authority if a
dispute or problem arises. "It's safer" alone, without a concrete
mechanism, doesn't fully answer the question (Lesson 12).
