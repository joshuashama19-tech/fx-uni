# Module 3 — Completion Checklist

## Before You Move On

Go through this honestly — check each box only if you could actually do
it right now, without flipping back to the lesson. Module 4 builds on
both this module and Module 2; it doesn't re-teach either.

### Core understanding

- [ ] I can explain what technical analysis is, and what it can and
      can't tell you.
- [ ] I can draw and interpret a basic trendline, and I know a single
      break doesn't automatically confirm a reversal.
- [ ] I understand what a moving average calculates, what its period
      means, and how it smooths price.
- [ ] I understand the tradeoff between shorter and longer moving-average
      periods.

### Momentum and indicators

- [ ] I can explain momentum in plain language, and I understand it's
      not the same thing as direction.
- [ ] I can interpret an RSI reading, including what overbought and
      oversold mean — and I understand neither one is an automatic
      buy/sell signal.
- [ ] I understand what MACD represents (the line, signal line, and
      histogram) and what a basic crossover or shrinking histogram
      suggests.
- [ ] I understand what a Fibonacci retracement level is, how it's
      calculated from a swing high and low, and why it's a reference
      zone, not a guaranteed turning point.

### Patterns and synthesis

- [ ] I can recognize a few common chart patterns (double top/bottom,
      head and shoulders, triangles) and explain why context matters for
      each.
- [ ] I can recognize a few common candlestick patterns (doji, hammer /
      hanging man, engulfing) and explain why the same shape can mean
      different things in different contexts.
- [ ] I can identify confluence in a realistic example, and I can
      explain why more confluence still doesn't mean certainty.
- [ ] I can explain the limitations every indicator in this module
      shares — that they're built from the past, they lag, they struggle
      in ranging markets, and they can disagree with each other.
- [ ] I understand why loading a chart with too many indicators usually
      adds noise rather than clarity.
- [ ] I can walk through a basic technical-analysis workflow — context,
      momentum, confluence, interpretation — on a new chart.

### Practice completed

- [ ] I completed all 12 exercises in `02-exercises.md`.
- [ ] I completed the 15-question checkpoint in `03-quiz.md` before
      looking at the answers.
- [ ] I checked my answers against `04-answer-key.md` and re-read any
      lesson connected to a question I missed.

### Mindset check

- [ ] I understand that no indicator, pattern, or combination of them in
      this module predicts the future or guarantees a trading outcome —
      every tool here describes the past and present, and supports an
      interpretation held with real uncertainty.
- [ ] I do not treat indicators as prediction machines, and I don't
      treat "more indicators" or "more confluence" as more certainty.

---

If every box above is checked honestly, you're ready for **Module 4 —
Price Action**. If anything is still unclear, that's a normal part of
learning this material — go back to the relevant lesson in
`01-content.md` before moving forward. There's no benefit to continuing
on a shaky foundation.
