# Module 3 — Answer Key

Kept in a separate file from `03-quiz.md` on purpose, so the quiz can be
worked through honestly before checking. Question numbers match exactly.

**1. Answer: B) Studying price data itself to inform interpretation.**
Technical analysis studies price, not news/economic data (that's
fundamental analysis, Module 5); it never guarantees an outcome (C); and
it doesn't replace risk management (D), which is still covered
separately in Module 6 (Lesson 1).

**2. Answer guide.** A trendline is a straight line connecting a series
of swing lows (in an uptrend) or swing highs (in a downtrend), extended
forward to visualize the trend and any levels price appears to be
respecting along the way (Lesson 2).

**3. Answer: 0.65360.** (0.65200 + 0.65350 + 0.65300 + 0.65450 +
0.65500) ÷ 5 = 3.26800 ÷ 5 = 0.65360 (Lesson 3).

**4. Answer: B) Smoother, but slower to reflect recent price changes.**
A long-period moving average filters out more noise but lags further
behind current price; it is not immune to lag (C), and period length
isn't tied to a specific chart type (D) (Lesson 3).

**5. Answer: False.** Momentum describes how strongly and quickly price
is moving, not just its direction — price can keep rising while momentum
is actually fading, which is exactly what RSI and MACD are built to help
notice (Lesson 4).

**6. Answer: changes +10, −15, −5, +15, +10 pips. Average gain = 7
pips. Average loss = 4 pips. RSI ≈ 63.6.** Gains: 10, 0, 0, 15, 10 → sum
35 ÷ 5 = 7. Losses: 0, 15, 5, 0, 0 → sum 20 ÷ 5 = 4. RS = 7 ÷ 4 = 1.75.
RSI = 100 − (100 ÷ (1 + 1.75)) = 100 − (100 ÷ 2.75) = 100 − 36.36 =
63.64 ≈ **63.6** (Lesson 5).

**7. Answer guide.** "Overbought" only means recent buying has been
strong relative to recent selling — it describes recent price behavior,
not a guaranteed turning point. A strong uptrend can push RSI above 70
and keep climbing for an extended stretch, so selling purely because of
that reading, without considering the broader trend and structure, is a
common and costly misread (Lesson 5).

**8. Answer: B) Momentum may be fading relative to the recent trend.** A
shrinking histogram means the gap between the MACD line and its signal
line is narrowing — it's a hint worth watching, not a guarantee of
reversal (A) and not a malfunction (C); histogram size does carry
meaning (ruling out D) (Lesson 6).

**9. Answer: 1.24618.** Level = high − (range × ratio) = 1.25000 −
((1.25000 − 1.24000) × 0.382) = 1.25000 − (0.01000 × 0.382) = 1.25000 −
0.00382 = **1.24618** (Lesson 7).

**10. Answer: Head and shoulders.** Three peaks with the middle one
("the head") higher than the two similar-level outer peaks ("the
shoulders"), commonly appearing at the end of an uptrend, is the
defining shape of a head and shoulders pattern (Lesson 8).

**11. Answer guide.** After a downtrend, this shape is commonly called a
**hammer**, sometimes read as a possible sign buyers are stepping in.
After an uptrend, the identical shape is commonly called a **hanging
man**, read differently — as a possible early sign sellers are showing
up. The shape alone doesn't determine the reading; what precedes it
(the context) does (Lesson 9).

**12. Answer: False.** Even strong confluence across multiple
independent observations makes an interpretation more coherent and worth
attention — it never guarantees the outcome. Some of these tools are
also naturally correlated rather than fully independent, and the market
can still do something that contradicts every tool on the chart at once
(Lesson 10).

**13. Answer guide.** Moving averages and MACD are both built from
averages of past closing prices, so by construction they always
summarize what has already happened rather than what's happening at this
exact instant — the longer the averaging period involved, the further
behind current price the reading trails. This matters because a
crossover or signal from these tools can arrive well after the price
move it's describing has already largely happened (Lesson 6, Lesson
11).

**14. Answer: B) Many indicators are correlated and tend to restate
similar information, adding visual noise instead of new insight.**
Charting platforms can display far more than three indicators (ruling
out A); indicators don't mathematically cancel out (C); and more
indicators does not reliably mean more confidence — often the opposite
(ruling out D) (Lesson 12).

**15. Answer guide.** A reasonable interpretation: the uptrend and an
elevated RSI both reflect real recent strength, but price is now
approaching a level with two independent reasons to expect a reaction
(resistance plus a Fibonacci level) while momentum (RSI 72) is already
elevated, leaving less room to extend before hitting resistance. A
trader following this module's workflow would treat this as a zone worth
watching closely for how price actually behaves there, not as a
confirmed signal to do anything in either direction — the uptrend could
continue through the level, or stall/reverse at it, and no tool in this
module can say which with certainty (Lesson 13).
