# Module 3 — Exercises

These exercises apply what you covered in `01-content.md`. They use new
numbers, not the exact examples from the lessons, so you're applying the
method, not recalling a result. Work through them in order. Answers
aren't provided here on purpose (as with Modules 1–2): this is practice,
not another quiz.

## Exercise 1 — Calculating moving averages

Six consecutive daily GBP/USD closes: 1.26100, 1.26250, 1.26200,
1.26400, 1.26550, 1.26500.

1. Calculate the 5-period SMA using the five most recent closes.
2. Calculate the 3-period SMA using the three most recent closes.
3. Is the most recent close trading above or below each of these
   averages? What does that suggest, on its own, using Lesson 3's
   "common uses" framing?

## Exercise 2 — Calculating a simplified RSI

Six consecutive 4-hour USD/JPY closes: 149.200, 149.350, 149.300,
149.150, 149.050, 148.950.

1. Calculate the five period-to-period changes, in pips.
2. Separate them into gains and losses, and calculate the average gain
   and average loss.
3. Calculate the RS ratio and the resulting RSI value.
4. Is this RSI reading closer to the "overbought" or "oversold"
   reference zone from Lesson 5? Does that automatically mean you should
   buy or sell? Explain why or why not.

## Exercise 3 — Simplified MACD interpretation

Six consecutive daily EUR/GBP closes: 1.31200, 1.31350, 1.31300,
1.31150, 1.31050, 1.30950.

1. Calculate the 3-period SMA (fast) and the 5-period SMA (slow).
2. Calculate the simplified MACD line value (fast − slow).
3. Is it positive or negative? What does that suggest about the
   short-term momentum relative to the longer-term average, per Lesson
   6?

## Exercise 4 — Fibonacci retracement calculation

AUD/USD swings from a low of 1.29500 up to a high of 1.30500.

1. Calculate the 38.2% retracement level.
2. Calculate the 61.8% retracement level.
3. If price pulls back and stalls almost exactly at your 61.8% answer,
   does that guarantee price will turn back upward from there? Explain
   your answer using Lesson 7.

## Exercise 5 — Identifying a chart pattern

A trader describes what they're watching: "Price rallied for two weeks,
then pulled back, then rallied again to a high just slightly above the
first one, then pulled back a second time to almost the same level as
the first pullback."

1. Which chart pattern from Lesson 8 does this most resemble?
2. Where would you expect this pattern to commonly appear (start of a
   trend, end of a trend, or inside a range), and why does that matter
   for how you'd interpret it?

## Exercise 6 — Same shape, different meaning

Two candles look identical: a small body near the top of the candle's
range, with a long lower wick and almost no upper wick.

1. Candle A appears after a multi-week downtrend. What would this
   candle commonly be called, and what might it suggest?
2. Candle B appears after a multi-week uptrend. What would this same
   shape commonly be called in this context, and what might it suggest?
3. In one sentence, explain why the same candle shape gets two different
   names and two different readings.

## Exercise 7 — Evaluating confluence

A trader lists their reasoning: "EUR/USD is in an uptrend. Price has
pulled back to a level that was resistance twice before and is now
acting as support. That level is also close to the 50% retracement of
the last big swing up. The most recent candle is a bullish engulfing
candle."

1. Identify the separate, independent pieces of evidence in this
   reasoning.
2. Does this amount to confluence, as defined in Lesson 10? Why or why
   not?
3. Does this combination guarantee the trade will work out? Explain,
   using Lesson 10's own caution about confluence.

## Exercise 8 — When indicators disagree

A trader sees GBP/USD trading above its 50-period moving average (a
bullish trend-filter reading) while RSI has fallen to 32 and MACD's
histogram has been shrinking for several periods.

1. Is this disagreement between tools a sign that something is broken,
   according to Lesson 11? Explain.
2. What would be a more honest way to describe this picture than
   forcing all three tools to "agree"?

## Exercise 9 — Reading a trendline

A trader draws a rising trendline connecting two swing lows on EUR/USD.
Price touches the line a third time and bounces upward again — but on
the fourth approach, price closes clearly below the line instead of
bouncing.

1. Does the first bounce (the third touch) make the trendline more or
   less credible as a level the market is respecting? Why?
2. Does the close below the trendline on the fourth approach
   automatically confirm the uptrend has reversed? Explain your answer
   using Lesson 2.

## Exercise 10 — Momentum vs. direction

A trader says: "USD/CAD is still making new highs, so momentum must
still be strong." Using Lesson 4 and Lesson 5, explain why this
statement could be wrong, and describe one way (mentioned in this
module) that a trader might notice momentum fading even while price is
still climbing.

## Exercise 11 — Spot the mistake

Each short scenario below contains one of the beginner mistakes covered
in this module. Identify the mistake and briefly explain what the
trader should have done differently.

1. "RSI hit 74, so I sold immediately."
2. "I added a moving average, then RSI, then MACD, then Fibonacci, then
   two more indicators, so I'd have the clearest possible picture."
3. "Everything lined up — trend, support, Fibonacci, and the candle — so
   I was sure the trade would work."
4. "I saw a hammer candle and bought, without checking whether it
   appeared after an uptrend or a downtrend."

## Exercise 12 — Applying the workflow

Using Lesson 13's four-step workflow, describe (in your own words, 4–6
sentences) how you would approach a chart showing: a clear downtrend on
the daily timeframe, an RSI reading of 25, and price approaching a
support zone that also sits near a 61.8% Fibonacci retracement level.
What would your interpretation be, and what would make that
interpretation wrong?
