# Module 3 — Knowledge Checkpoint

15 questions covering everything in Module 3. Mixed format: multiple
choice, true/false, short answer, interpretation scenarios, and
practical calculations. Write down your answers before checking
`04-answer-key.md` — checking as you go defeats the point of a
checkpoint.

**1. Multiple choice.** Technical analysis is primarily concerned with:
A) Studying news and economic data to predict price
B) Studying price data itself to inform interpretation
C) Guaranteeing the outcome of a trade before entering it
D) Replacing the need to understand risk management

**2. Short answer.** What is a trendline, and what does it connect?

**3. Calculation.** Five consecutive daily AUD/USD closes: 0.65200,
0.65350, 0.65300, 0.65450, 0.65500. Calculate the 5-period SMA.

**4. Multiple choice.** Compared to a short-period moving average, a
long-period moving average is generally:
A) Faster to react, but noisier
B) Smoother, but slower to reflect recent price changes
C) Immune to lag entirely
D) Only usable on daily charts

**5. True or False.** Momentum and direction are the same thing — if
price is rising, momentum must be strong.

**6. Calculation.** Six consecutive 1-hour EUR/USD closes: 1.09800,
1.09900, 1.09750, 1.09700, 1.09850, 1.09950. Calculate the five
period-to-period changes, the average gain, the average loss, and the
resulting RSI.

**7. Short answer.** Why doesn't an RSI reading above 70 automatically
mean a trader should sell?

**8. Multiple choice.** In MACD, a shrinking histogram, even while price
continues in the same direction, most likely suggests:
A) The trend is guaranteed to reverse immediately
B) Momentum may be fading relative to the recent trend
C) The MACD indicator is malfunctioning
D) Nothing — histogram size has no meaning

**9. Calculation.** GBP/USD swings from a low of 1.24000 to a high of
1.25000. Calculate the 38.2% Fibonacci retracement level.

**10. Interpretation scenario.** A trader describes a chart: three
peaks, where the middle peak is clearly higher than the two outer peaks,
and the two outer peaks sit at roughly similar levels to each other,
appearing after a long uptrend. Which chart pattern does this describe?

**11. Short answer.** A candle with a small body near the top of its
range and a long lower wick appears after a downtrend, and the same
shape appears after an uptrend on a different chart. What is each
occurrence commonly called, and why does the same shape get different
names?

**12. True or False.** If trend context, a support level, a Fibonacci
level, and a candlestick pattern all point the same direction, the trade
is guaranteed to work.

**13. Short answer.** Why do moving averages and MACD lag current price,
and why does that matter?

**14. Multiple choice.** Why does loading a chart with many indicators
at once usually create problems rather than solve them?
A) Most charting platforms can't display more than three indicators
B) Many indicators are correlated and tend to restate similar
   information, adding visual noise instead of new insight
C) Indicators automatically cancel each other out mathematically
D) It's not actually a problem — more indicators always means more
   confidence

**15. Interpretation scenario.** A chart shows a clear uptrend on the
daily timeframe, RSI reading 72, and price approaching a resistance
level that also sits near a Fibonacci retracement level from a prior
swing. Using the workflow from Lesson 13, describe what a reasonable —
not certain — interpretation of this picture would be.
