# Module 6 — Completion Checklist

## Before You Move On

Go through this honestly — check each box only if you could actually run
the calculation right now, without flipping back to the lesson. Module 7
builds on the discipline this module teaches and assumes you can already
run these numbers comfortably.

### Core calculations

- [ ] I can calculate the maximum dollar risk on a trade from an account
      size and a chosen risk percentage.
- [ ] I can calculate position size from a risk amount, a stop-loss
      distance in pips, and a pip-value assumption.
- [ ] I understand why pip value depends on the currency pair, position
      size, account currency, and exchange rate — not one universal
      number.

### Stop loss, take profit, and risk/reward

- [ ] I understand a stop loss as a predefined invalidation point, and I
      can explain why moving it further away or removing it changes the
      trade's actual risk.
- [ ] I can explain slippage in plain terms, and I understand a stop
      loss does not always execute at exactly the requested price.
- [ ] I can calculate a risk-to-reward ratio from a risk amount and a
      reward amount.
- [ ] I can calculate the breakeven win rate for a given risk-to-reward
      ratio, and explain what it means.

### Expectancy

- [ ] I can calculate expectancy from a win rate and average win/loss,
      using the formula from Lesson 7.
- [ ] I understand expectancy as a long-run mathematical average, not a
      promise about any individual trade.
- [ ] I understand that a high win rate does not, by itself, guarantee a
      positive expectancy.

### Drawdown and losing streaks

- [ ] I can calculate the percentage gain required to recover from a
      given percentage loss.
- [ ] I understand why recovery requirements grow disproportionately
      larger as losses get bigger.
- [ ] I can calculate how a fixed percentage risk affects an account
      balance through a losing streak.
- [ ] I understand that a losing streak doesn't automatically mean a
      strategy is broken, and doesn't justify increasing risk, revenge
      trading, or abandoning my rules.

### Leverage, margin, and risk

- [ ] I can distinguish leverage, margin, and risk as three separate
      concepts.
- [ ] I can calculate the margin required to open a position from its
      notional value and the leverage in use.
- [ ] I understand that leverage and margin affect how much capital is
      required to open a position, but do not by themselves determine my
      planned risk.

### Planned vs. actual, and personal rules

- [ ] I can distinguish planned risk from actual realized loss, and I
      understand the roles slippage, spread, and commission play in that
      difference.
- [ ] I can fill in a complete, specific set of personal risk rules
      (maximum risk per trade, daily/weekly loss limits, exposure limits,
      stop-loss and position-sizing rules, stop-trading conditions, and a
      review schedule) suited to my own situation.

### Practice completed

- [ ] I completed all 15 exercises in `02-exercises.md`.
- [ ] I completed the 17-question checkpoint in `03-quiz.md` before
      looking at the answers.
- [ ] I checked my answers against `04-answer-key.md` and re-read any
      lesson connected to a question I missed.

### Mindset check

- [ ] I understand that no risk percentage, position-sizing formula, or
      risk-to-reward ratio in this module guarantees profitability —
      every calculation here controls what a trade can cost, never
      whether it wins.
- [ ] I have not adopted any single risk percentage or rule from this
      module as a mandatory universal standard, and I understand these
      values are decisions suited to my own capital and circumstances.
- [ ] I understand that capital preservation is the first goal, and that
      protecting an account from a single oversized loss matters more
      than any individual trade's outcome.

---

If every box above is checked honestly, you're ready for **Module 7 —
Trading Psychology**. If anything is still unclear, that's a normal part
of learning this material — go back to the relevant lesson in
`01-content.md` before moving forward. The calculations in this module
are the practical foundation the rest of the course assumes you have.
