# Module 6 — Knowledge Checkpoint

17 questions covering everything in Module 6. Mixed format: multiple
choice, true/false, short answer, and calculations. A calculator is
genuinely useful here. Write down your answers before checking
`04-answer-key.md` — checking as you go defeats the point of a
checkpoint.

**1. Multiple choice.** Risk management is best described as:

A) A method that guarantees a trading strategy will be profitable
B) The set of decisions and calculations that control how much capital
   is exposed to loss on a trade or across an account
C) A way to eliminate all possible losses
D) Only relevant for traders with large accounts

**2. Short answer.** List the five steps in the core chain from Lesson 1,
in order, starting with account size and ending with position size.

**3. Calculation.** Account = $4,000. Risk per trade = 1.25%. Calculate
the maximum dollar risk.

**4. True or False.** This course recommends a single universal risk
percentage (such as 1%) as the only acceptable choice for every trader.

**5. True or False.** A stop loss guarantees that a losing trade will
close at exactly the price requested, with no possible difference under
any market condition.

**6. Short answer.** Name at least three factors that determine a
currency pair's actual pip value.

**7. Calculation.** Account = $1,800. Risk = 1%. Stop loss = 30 pips.
Pip value assumption = $1 per pip. Calculate the dollar risk and the
position size in mini lots.

**8. Calculation.** A trader risks $25 and sets a target that would
produce a $75 gain if reached. Calculate the risk-to-reward ratio.

**9. Calculation.** Using Lesson 6's breakeven win rate formula, calculate
the breakeven win rate for a 1:2.5 risk-to-reward ratio.

**10. Calculation.** Win rate = 50%. Average win = $35. Loss rate = 50%.
Average loss = $20. Calculate the expectancy per trade.

**11. True or False.** A strategy with a high win rate is always
profitable, regardless of the size of its average win compared to its
average loss.

**12. Calculation.** An account experiences a 40% drawdown. Calculate the
percentage gain required to fully recover to the original balance.

**13. Calculation.** A $4,000 account risks a fixed 1% per trade and
suffers three consecutive losses (risk recalculated from the current
balance each time). Calculate the account balance after each loss, and
the total drawdown as a percentage of the original $4,000.

**14. True or False.** A losing streak, on its own, proves that a trading
strategy is broken and must be abandoned.

**15. Short answer.** In your own words, distinguish leverage, margin,
and risk — the three concepts Lesson 10 is built around.

**16. Calculation.** A trader opens a position of 200,000 units (2
standard lots) on a pair trading at an exchange rate of 1.25000, using
1:50 leverage. Calculate the notional value and the margin required.

**17. Short answer.** Name at least two reasons the actual realized loss
on a trade can differ slightly from the planned risk calculated in
advance.
