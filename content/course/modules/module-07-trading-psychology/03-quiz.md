# Module 7 — Knowledge Checkpoint

17 questions covering everything in Module 7. Mixed format: multiple
choice, true/false, short answer, and scenario-based questions. These
questions test whether you can actually apply the module's frameworks to
a realistic situation, not just recall a definition. Write down your
answers before checking `04-answer-key.md` — checking as you go defeats
the point of a checkpoint.

**1. Multiple choice.** Trading psychology, as taught in this module, is
best described as:

A) A way to eliminate emotion from trading entirely
B) The study of how emotion, habit, and decision-making affect whether a
   trader actually follows their own process
C) A guarantee that disciplined traders will be profitable
D) A replacement for the risk-management calculations in Module 6

**2. Short answer.** Per Lesson 1, explain the difference between having
a losing trade and making a bad decision. Can a trade lose money and
still have been a good decision?

**3. Scenario.** A trader has just closed two losing trades that both
followed their process correctly. A new setup forms that meets every one
of their stated entry criteria, but they hesitate and let it pass without
a specific new reason. Which pattern from Lesson 2 does this describe?

**4. Scenario.** A trader's position reaches its planned take-profit
level. Instead of closing it, they move the target further away because
the trend "still looks strong," with no new structural evidence. Which
pattern from Lesson 3 does this describe?

**5. Multiple choice.** FOMO, as defined in Lesson 4, is:

A) A calculation error in position sizing
B) The urge to enter a trade specifically because a move is already
   happening, driven by the discomfort of not being part of it
C) A type of cognitive bias related to memory
D) A synonym for revenge trading

**6. Short answer.** Explain why missing one specific setup is not the
same as "missing the market," per Lesson 4.

**7. Scenario.** A trader's properly sized trade hits its stop loss. They
immediately take another trade that only loosely matches their criteria,
at a larger position size than normal, specifically to recover the first
loss. Name this pattern, and explain what should have happened instead
per Lesson 5.

**8. Multiple choice.** Per Lesson 6's practical filter, a setup deserves
consideration when:

A) It has been a long time since the last trade
B) It would meet the trader's criteria even if they had taken zero other
   trades that day
C) The market feels unusually quiet
D) Other setups nearby also look marginal

**9. Short answer.** Choose one bias from Lesson 7 (other than
confirmation bias) and explain: what it is, how it shows up in trading,
and one practical countermeasure.

**10. Scenario.** A trader is convinced a pair is heading higher. As
price consolidates, they notice every small bounce as confirmation, while
a lower high forming at the same time goes unmentioned in their own
reasoning. Name this bias, and describe the countermeasure from Lesson 7.

**11. True or False.** A planned loss and a careless loss are the same
thing, as long as the dollar amount lost is identical.

**12. Short answer.** In your own words, define discipline as Lesson 9
uses the term. Give one example of a disciplined decision and one example
of an impulsive decision in the same situation.

**13. True or False.** Following a consistent trading process guarantees
consistent trading results.

**14. Short answer.** List at least four of the six questions in Lesson
9's Pre-Trade Psychology Check.

**15. Scenario.** A trader entered a trade that matched their criteria,
but moved their stop loss further away mid-trade out of discomfort, and
the trade closed at a larger loss than planned. Using Lesson 12's
Post-Trade Review framework, name at least three of the seven questions
this trader should ask themselves.

**16. True or False.** A winning streak is a period when a trader's usual
risk and rule discipline matters less, since recent results have been
good.

**17. Short answer.** Name the six column headers of Lesson 14's
Rule-Violation Log.
